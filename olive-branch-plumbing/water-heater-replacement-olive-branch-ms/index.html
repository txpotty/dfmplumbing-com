    Electric Water Heater Replacement in Olive Branch

    Electric water heaters in Olive Branch usually fail from a burned-out heating element, a bad thermostat or a tank that has finally rusted through at the bottom. Elements are cheap to swap. A leaking tank cannot, so if water is coming from the tank body itself, we replace the heater and match the voltage, wattage and breaker size to what your panel already supplies. Most swaps take a few hours.

    Conventional Tank Water Heater Replacement in Olive Branch

    Standard tank heaters remain the most common type, and they usually fail from age, rust or sediment. A leak at the base, loud popping or rusty hot water means it is time, and a like-for-like or resized swap usually takes two to four hours. You shower tonight.

    Bradford White Water Heater Services in Olive Branch

    We use Bradford White often on our jobs because the tanks hold up and the parts are easy to find.     Rheem and AO Smith Water Heater Replacement in Olive Branch, MS

    Rheem and AO Smith make tanks in nearly every size, from 30-gallon short tanks to 80-gallon tall models. Both are solid, and the pick usually depends on what fits the space, the warranty length you want and what the supply house has in stock. We help you compare.

    Tankless Water Heater Upgrades in Olive Branch

    Going tankless when the old tank fails is common, but it is a bigger job than a tank swap. A whole-home tankless unit can draw four times the gas of a 40-gallon tank, so the gas line from the meter usually has to be upsized, and the old metal vent gets replaced with stainless or PVC run to the maker's specs. We price both options.

    Water Heater Replacement Cost Comparison in Olive Branch, MS

    A standard tank replacement costs the least up front in Olive Branch, MS. Tankless and hybrid units cost more up front but can lower operating costs, and tankless often lasts 15 to 20 years versus about 8 to 12 for a tank. Ask for each option.

    Upsizing Your Water Heater in Olive Branch

    Running out of hot water usually means the house outgrew the tank. A bigger tank or a higher first-hour rating handles more back-to-back showers, and sometimes a 50-gallon high-recovery unit matches a 75 in the same footprint. Bigger is not always needed.
