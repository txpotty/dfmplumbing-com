    No Hot Water Tankless Repair in Olive Branch

    No hot water at all usually points to ignition, gas supply or a heat exchanger choked with scale. We start with the error history in the service menu, then test the igniter, flame rod and gas pressure in that order, because a weak flame signal and low pressure can look identical on the display.     Commercial Tankless Water Heater Repair in Olive Branch

    Restaurants and laundromats in Olive Branch often run two to six tankless units on a common manifold. When one unit drops out, the rest carry the load and scale up sooner, so we check the cascade controller, the isolation valves on each heater and the water quality coming in. Downtime costs money. We schedule around service hours when we can.

    Navien Tankless Water Heater Repair in Olive Branch

    Navien condensing units run a second stainless exchanger that pulls heat from the exhaust. That makes condensate, and a plugged trap or a full neutralizer will shut the heater down with a drain or flue error. We clear the trap, check the PVC vent pitch and test the circulation pump on NPE-A models with built-in recirculation. Then we fire it at low and high flow.

    Commercial Tankless Repair in Olive Branch, MS

    Hotels and gyms draw hot water from open to close, so their tankless racks scale up two or three times faster than a home unit. We service rack systems with manifold kits, pulling the fault log and testing the flow sensor on every heater in the rack, and set a descaling schedule by run hours. Quarterly is common.

    Electric Tankless Water Heater Repair in Olive Branch, MS

    Whole-house electric tankless units pull a lot of current, often 100 to 150 amps on two or three dedicated breakers. When a breaker trips under load, the cause can be a shorted element or a loose, scorched lug, and both get tested with a meter before anything is replaced. Element resistance and supply voltage get measured. Scorched wire gets cut back.

    Condensing Tankless Repair in Olive Branch, MS

    Condensing tankless heaters pull extra heat out of the exhaust and make acidic condensate that has to drain somewhere. That condensate runs through a trap and, where code or the manual calls for it, a neutralizer filled with limestone or marble chips before it reaches the drain. Clogged traps shut the unit down. We clean the trap, refill the neutralizer and check the PVC vent for sag or the wrong slope.
