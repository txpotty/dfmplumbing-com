    No-Water Calls in Pascagoula

    Pump trouble rarely gives notice. Usually the faucet spits air, then quits, after the motor has been limping along for weeks. We test the control box and pressure switch before pulling anything.

    Well Pump Brands We Install in Pascagoula, MS

    Goulds, Grundfos, Franklin Electric and Sta-Rite cover nearly every well pump we install. Each brand has strengths, and we choose based on your well. Parts for Goulds and Franklin are easy to get, which matters when a control box dies on a weekend.

    Well Pump Electrical Troubleshooting in Pascagoula, MS

    No-water calls often end at the control box, not the pump. On a three-wire submersible, the start capacitor or the relay in that box fails far more often than the motor down the well does. We test them first. A $60 part beats a $2,500 pump pull.

    Submersible Well Pump Replacement in Pascagoula

    A submersible pump hangs on drop pipe inside a 4-inch or 6-inch casing, often 100 to 400 feet down, so pulling one is a two-person job with a pump hoist. Most fail from sand chewing up the impellers or a lightning surge that cooks the windings. Before pulling, we test the windings with a megohmmeter. That tells us whether the problem is down the hole or up top.

    Grundfos Well Pump Replacement in Pascagoula, MS

    Grundfos SQ and SQE pumps have a built-in soft start and dry-run protection, and they run on two wires with no control box at the surface. They are pricier than a standard pump. Add the CU 301 controller to an SQE and you get constant pressure.

    Constant Pressure Pump Systems in Pascagoula

    Franklin SubDrive and Grundfos SQE are the two constant pressure systems we install most. Both use a small 2 to 20 gallon tank instead of a big one, since the drive keeps the pump running at a matched speed. The soft start is easier on the motor. Expect to pay more up front.

    Jet Pump Replacement in Pascagoula, MS

    Jet pumps sit above ground, in a well house or basement, and pull water up by suction from wells where the water level is within about 25 feet. A two-pipe deep-well jet can reach further down at the cost of efficiency. Lost prime is the classic complaint. Most of the time a bad foot valve or a suction leak is letting water drain back, and we check those before condemning the pump.
