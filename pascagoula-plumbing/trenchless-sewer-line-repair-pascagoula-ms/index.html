    Pipe Bursting Sewer Line Replacements in Pascagoula

    A bursting head is a steel cone, slightly wider than the old pipe, pulled through the line by a hydraulic unit or a winch cable. As it moves, it splits the old clay or cast iron and shoves the pieces into the surrounding soil while a string of new HDPE pipe follows right behind it. Fused joints, no gaps. We use it when the old line is too broken or flattened to hold a liner.

    Trenchless Drain Pipe Lining in Pascagoula, MS

    Smaller drain lines inside the house can be lined too, including 2-inch kitchen drains and the 3-inch cast iron stacks found in many older homes. We work through cleanouts and roof vents, so the drywall and tile stay where they are. After curing, a small robotic cutter reopens each branch connection from inside the pipe. That saves opening a finished wall.

    Compare total cost, not just the plumbing line on the invoice.     Perma-Liner Trenchless Solutions in Pascagoula

    Perma-Liner makes inversion drums and epoxy systems for lining 2- to 6-inch drain lines, which covers most of what we see in houses. The equipment fits in a pickup bed. Compact gear matters on tight lots. The resin is mixed on site, so it does not start curing before the liner is in place.

    Pipe Bursting in Pascagoula, MS

    Two pits are standard for a burst, one at the house end near the foundation and one out near the street or the city tap. Before pulling, the crew heat-fuses sections of HDPE into one continuous length on the ground. The winch or hydraulic puller then drags the bursting head and the new pipe through. A 60-foot pull often takes under an hour.

    Cured-In-Place Pipe Prep and Lining in Pascagoula

    Prep is most of the job. We jet the line at high pressure to strip roots, grease and loose scale, then camera it again to measure the exact footage before cutting the liner tube to length. Skip that step and the liner can wrinkle or fail to bond. Lining works best on cracked or root-intruded lines that still hold their round shape.

    Cast Iron Sewer Line Repair in Pascagoula, MS

    Cast iron rusts from the inside out, and the bottom of the pipe goes first. Plumbers call that channel rot, a groove that eats through the bottom. We descale the pipe with chain knockers or carbide cutters spinning on a flexible shaft, which brings the wall back to bare metal so a liner can bond to it. Under a slab, that beats jackhammering.
