    Septic Pipe Repair in Tupelo, MS

    Old homes may still have clay tile or Orangeburg pipe running to the tank, and both fail badly with age.     Septic Lid and Riser Repair in Tupelo

    Old concrete lids crack, and a cracked lid over a 1,000-gallon tank is a real danger to kids and pets. We replace failing lids and add risers that bring access up to the lawn. Future pumping gets cheaper. Every lid we set is screwed down or locked so a child cannot lift it.

    Septic Tank Leak Repair in Tupelo

    A tank that never fills to the outlet, or one that fills slowly with groundwater after rain, has a leak. After pumping, we inspect the walls and the joints with a strong light, and a dye test in the drains shows where water comes in or goes out. Hairline cracks get a hydraulic cement patch. Split tanks get replaced.

    Inlet and Outlet Baffle Replacement in Tupelo, MS

    Baffles hold back the scum layer on top and the sludge on the bottom so only clear effluent leaves the tank. Concrete ones corrode from the acid that sewer gas forms and eventually fall off into the tank. Once the outlet baffle is gone, solids head straight for the field. We replace them with PVC sanitary tees. Usually a short job.

    Effluent Pump Replacement in Tupelo, MS

    Effluent pumps in a dosing chamber last roughly 8 to 15 years, and they usually quit without much warning at all. Humming with no flow points to a jammed impeller or a failed capacitor. Our tech pulls the old unit and checks the discharge before the new one goes in. Floats get replaced at the same time.

    Drain Field Line Jetting in Tupelo

    Distribution laterals clog from the inside with solids and slime, especially after years without pumping. A jetter goes into each lateral from the D-box or an end cleanout, and what washes back shows how plugged it was. Roots get cut. If the soil itself has sealed, jetting will not fix it, and we say so.

    Leach Field Restoration in Tupelo, MS

    Before we condemn a leach field, we open the distribution box and look at how evenly it splits the flow. A tilted or crushed D-box can send everything to one trench and drown it. A new or leveled box brings a lot of fields back. Some fields are past saving. For those, we explain the design and permit steps.
