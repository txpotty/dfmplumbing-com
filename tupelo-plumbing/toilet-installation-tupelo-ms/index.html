    Floor-Mount and Wall-Hung Toilet Installs in Tupelo, MS

    Two-Piece Toilet Installation in Tupelo

    Two-piece toilets come with the tank and bowl separate, joined by a rubber gasket and two or three bolts. We assemble them on the floor before setting. Tightening the tank bolts evenly, side to side, matters more than how hard they go, because an uneven pull cracks the china or leaves a slow drip at one bolt. It should not wobble.

    Wall-Hung Toilet Installation in Tupelo, MS

    Wall-hung toilets need a wall at least 2 by 6 deep, or a furred-out chase built in front of an existing 2 by 4 wall. The floor underneath stays open. Cleaning gets easier, and the bowl height can be set anywhere within the carrier range, useful for a tall family. Service happens through the flush plate.

    ADA-Compliant Toilet Installation in Tupelo

    ADA toilets put the seat 17 to 19 inches off the floor, and the centerline sits 16 to 18 inches from the side wall. It is set that way so a grab bar fits. Before anything is ordered we measure the drain, because shifting it to meet the ADA centerline means cutting into the floor. Elongated bowls are standard here.

    Toilet Brands We Install in Tupelo

    We install Kohler, TOTO, American Standard, Gerber and most other brands sold at supply houses and home centers. Each one has its own parts quirks. Some one-piece TOTO models mount with a Unifit adapter, and certain Kohler toilets use a tank-to-bowl gasket that most hardware stores do not carry, so we stock both. We keep those parts on hand.

    Wall-Mounted Toilet Installation in Tupelo

    Wall-mounted toilets hang off a steel carrier bolted to the floor inside the wall. The tank sits behind an access plate. Because the carrier carries all the weight, we anchor its feet to the slab or framing first and check it for plumb before the drywall or tile goes up. Plan on a remodel, not a swap.

    Commercial Toilet Installation in Tupelo, MS

    Commercial restrooms take a beating, so we install vitreous china bowls with flushometer valves rather than tank toilets. A flushometer needs a 1 inch supply and steady pressure. We confirm the building line can deliver the valve's rated flow, often 20 to 25 gallons a minute, before any fixture goes up. Fixture counts follow code.

    Standard Toilet Installation in Tupelo

    A standard floor-mount toilet is a two-hour job when the flange is sound. The stop gets shut, the tank drained, and the old bowl lifted out. After scraping the old wax off, we check the flange for cracks, set new stainless bolts, and lower the new toilet straight down onto a fresh seal so it compresses evenly. The old one leaves with us.

    Pressure-Assisted Toilet Installation in Tupelo

    Pressure-assisted toilets have a sealed vessel inside the tank that uses line pressure to push the flush. They need at least 20 psi to work right. Before suggesting one, we put a gauge on a hose bib, and we mention the noise, because the flush carries through the whole house. They rarely clog.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Installation in Tupelo

    Most homeowners in Tupelo, MS pay $200 to $500 to have a toilet installed, fixture not included. A stubborn old toilet adds time. Bolts rusted solid have to be cut with a hacksaw or oscillating tool, and a cast iron flange that breaks during removal needs a repair ring, each of which adds labor.

    It depends on the floor and the flange. A straight swap on a solid flange is a reasonable DIY job. New tile, a low or broken flange, a wall-hung carrier, or a toilet that has to move are the jobs where a plumber saves you money, because mistakes there end up under the floor.

    Lower water bills come first. An old 3.5 gallon toilet uses almost three times the water of a 1.28 gallon model. Modern bowls clog less thanks to wider glazed trapways, and the smoother porcelain is easier to keep clean.

    Yes, our labor is guaranteed. A leak caused by the install gets fixed at no charge. The toilet itself carries the manufacturer's warranty, usually one to five years on parts and often much longer on the vitreous china, depending on the brand and model. Keep the box label for the model number.

    Cracks in the tank or bowl are the clearest sign. Hairline cracks can be hard to spot. Look for a slow drip on the floor that comes back after you dry it, or frequent clogs on an early low-flow model with a narrow trapway. So is a toilet you have rebuilt twice in three years.

    Start with the rough-in, since that decides which models fit at all. Next comes bowl shape. Elongated bowls are more comfortable but stick out about two inches farther, which can be a problem in a small half bath with the door swinging in. Check the MaP flush score too.

    We guarantee the workmanship on every toilet we set. A leak from our work gets fixed free. That covers the seal and the supply connection, while manufacturing defects in the toilet fall under the maker's warranty, which we help you file if a part fails early. Hang on to your receipt.
