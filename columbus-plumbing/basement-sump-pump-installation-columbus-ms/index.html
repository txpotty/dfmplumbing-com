    Discharge Line and Check Valve Installation in Columbus

    Rigid PVC carries the water from the pump out through the rim joist and at least 10 feet from the house. We add a spring check valve so the water in the pipe cannot fall back, which cuts motor run time and the loud thunk you hear after every cycle. Exposed pipes in crawlspaces, outdoor spigots and well equipment are the most vulnerable during a rare deep cold snap. Where lines can freeze, we fit a freeze-resistant outlet.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Systems in Columbus

    Outages and heavy rain tend to show up together. Without house power, a battery backup keeps a second pump going, and a fully charged 75 amp-hour battery can run a typical backup pump for several hours of on-and-off cycling, longer on a light night. We test it under load.

    Residential Basement Sump Pump Installation in Columbus

    A typical house pit holds an 18 by 22 inch basin with a 1/3 horsepower pump, and that covers most homes. Our plumbers size the unit to the water coming in and the height it has to climb, not to the sale sticker. In Columbus, we set the basin at the low corner, bed it in washed gravel and run 1-1/2 inch PVC out through the rim joist. Discharge gets carried far enough out that it cannot soak back toward the wall.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Installation in Columbus

    Pedestal pumps put the motor on a column above the water. That keeps it dry and easy to reach, which is why they suit narrow pits under 14 inches across, and why they often outlast cheap submersibles in Columbus homes. They are louder, though.

    Sump Pit Installation in Columbus, MS

    Pit size controls how often the pump starts. Too small a pit means the pump short cycles in heavy flow, so we usually install an 18 by 22 inch or 18 by 30 inch basin with a solid bottom and perforated sides wrapped in filter fabric. Fewer starts mean a longer life.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Columbus

    Figure on roughly $800 to $2,000 for most basement sump pump installs in Columbus.     Changing out a pump in an existing pit takes about two to three hours. Plan on most of a day for a new pit with a slab cut and patch, since the concrete needs a little time to firm up before the pump goes into service.

    Newer houses and manufactured homes mostly use PEX or CPVC supply lines with PVC drains. Slab homes without a basement rarely need a pump. Houses with basements or below-grade rooms are another story, since they can take on water fast during heavy storms, especially where the ground sheds rain rather than soaking it up. A pit costs less than a cleanup.

    For some homeowners, a same-model pump swap is a fair weekend job. New pits and backup systems are a different matter, because those mistakes end in flooding, cracked slabs or electrical hazards, and fixing them costs more than paying for the install up front. Leave those to us.

    Swapping an identical plug-in pump is doable for a handy homeowner.
