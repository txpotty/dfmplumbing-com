    Replacing a Collapsed Sewer by Trench in Columbus

    Collapsed pipe cannot be burst or lined. When the camera stops at a pile of broken clay or a flattened section of Orangeburg, the only fix is to dig down, clear the debris and lay new pipe through that stretch. Digging also lets us see the soil around the break. Often there is a void or a root mat right at the failure point.

    Open-Trench Sewer Replacement in Columbus

    A collapsed run or a long belly usually means we dig. The crew trenches the full length of the lateral, removes the failed pipe and sets new PVC on compacted bedding with steady fall. Where we can, the sod is cut and set aside before the digging starts. The inspector sees open pipe. We compact the backfill in layers, which keeps a dip from forming along the trench.

    Keeping a New Sewer Line Healthy in Columbus

    New pipe still needs a little care. Plant thirsty trees away from the line, since big roots can push on even a sound pipe. Slab and crawlspace foundations are typical, and soil movement under a slab can shift drain lines and supply pipes. Wipes do not break down. A camera check every few years on an older house catches problems at the city tap or the cleanout early.

    Sewer Permits and Code in Columbus, MS

    Sewer replacement nearly always needs a permit. Our office files the permit and books the inspection, and our crew meets the inspector so you do not have to. The inspector looks at pipe size, slope, bedding and cleanouts. Unpermitted sewer work can show up on a home inspection when you sell.

    Upgrading Old Sewer Pipe Materials in Columbus

    Clay, cast iron and Orangeburg all fail in their own ways. Clay joints let roots in every few feet, cast iron scales and rusts through along the bottom, and Orangeburg tar paper pipe slowly flattens into an oval. PVC and HDPE do none of that. HDPE is fused into one continuous piece with no joints for roots to find.

    Commercial Sewer Line Replacement in Columbus

    Restaurants and strip centers cannot close for a week. We plan commercial sewer replacements around business hours, stage the equipment the night before and often work after close so the line is back in service by opening. Expect six inch pipe, sometimes larger. Grease and heavy flow change how we pick the material and set the cleanouts.

    How Pipe Bursting Works in Columbus

    A winch pulls the bursting head. Because the head is a little wider than the old pipe, it cracks it into the surrounding dirt as the new HDPE follows. You get a pit at each end. The fused pipe has no joints, so roots cannot get in again.

    Clay and Orangeburg Sewer Replacement in Columbus, MS

    Orangeburg is tar-soaked wood fiber. Many mid-century houses got it, and by now most of it has blistered and flattened enough to snag waste. Clay is tougher but leaks roots. Several bad spots on one run usually make full replacement the better buy.
