    Slab Leaks From Ground Movement in Columbus

    Pipe set in concrete cannot flex when the ground moves, so it cracks at elbows and where it rises through the slab. Low, wet ground in many areas also means poor drainage, which matters for sewer lines and septic fields alike. We isolate the line first. Then acoustic listening and thermal imaging find the leak without breaking up the floor to look, and you get a marked spot plus repair options before any concrete gets cut.

    Water Line Leak Detection in Columbus, MS

    Supply leaks hide inside walls, above ceilings and under yards. The bill usually tells you first.     Commercial Building Leaks in Columbus, MS

    Fast Leak Response in Columbus

    Preventive Leak Inspections in Columbus, MS

    A yearly leak check catches small problems early. We test static pressure, read the meter with everything off and look at supply lines, water heater fittings and shutoff valves. Older homes with galvanized pipe or original water heater connectors get the most out of this, because those parts often fail together. Short visit, big savings.

    Underground Water Line Leak Detection in Columbus, MS

    The service line from the meter to the house can leak for months without surfacing, especially in old galvanized pipe. A green stripe in a dry yard is a hint. We trace the line with an electronic locator, then use a ground microphone to hear the leak, and on plastic pipe we inject tracer gas and sniff for it at the surface.

    Leaks in Walls and Ceilings in Columbus, MS

    A brown ring on the ceiling under a bathroom usually points to a shower valve, a drain joint or a toilet wax ring. Our job is figuring out which. Thermal cameras show the cool wet area, a moisture meter confirms it and a borescope through a small hole lets us see the actual pipe before any big opening is made.

    PEX and Copper Leak Detection in Columbus, MS

    On a ground microphone, copper leaks are easy to hear. PEX is quieter, since plastic pipe does not carry sound well, so we lean more on thermal scans, moisture readings and tracer gas when the house is plumbed in it. We handle both every week. Pinholes in copper and failed crimp fittings in PEX are the two leaks we find most.
