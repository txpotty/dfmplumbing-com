    Propane Water Heater Repair in Columbus

    Propane and natural gas heaters look the same but burn at different pressures. Propane units need a matching orifice and a control set near 11 inches of water column. Natural gas runs closer to 7 inches, so a heater converted badly, or a regulator that drifted, gives you soot, a yellow flame and a pilot that keeps going out for no obvious reason. A manometer reading tells us the supply pressure. Wrong parts get swapped for the right ones.

    Pilot Light & Burner Issues in Columbus

    A pilot that lights, holds for ten seconds and then goes out is almost always a thermocouple or a weak magnet in the gas valve. No flame at all usually means a plugged pilot orifice or a bad piezo. Main burner problems show up another way. The pilot stays lit but the main flame never comes on, which points to the thermostat side of the valve, a tripped thermal cutoff or a flame arrestor so clogged with lint that the burner starves for air. Each part gets tested in that sequence.

    Burner Cleaning and Repair in Columbus, MS

    Yellow flame tips, soot on the burner door or a roaring sound at startup all mean the burner is not getting clean air. We pull the burner, brush the ports and blow out the orifice. We also confirm the orifice is drilled for the fuel you have, because a natural gas orifice on a propane heater, or the reverse, will soot up the flue and push carbon monoxide levels well past normal. A good flame is blue and steady. Rust flakes on the burner tell us to look up the flue.

    Burner Assembly and Venting Repair in Columbus

    Rust scale on the burner tray is a sign the flue baffle or the tank top is shedding. The burner comes out, the ports get cleared and the flame arrestor gets a look.     A thermocouple is a small copper-sheathed rod that sits in the pilot flame and makes a tiny electric current. That current holds a magnet in the gas valve open. No flame, no current. When the pilot goes out, the magnet lets go and the valve shuts off gas to both the pilot and the burner, which is the safety that keeps raw gas from filling a closet. Weak thermocouples cause more pilot outages than anything else.

    Part replacement typically does not. Swapping a thermocouple or gas valve is a repair in most places. Most permits add only a few days.
