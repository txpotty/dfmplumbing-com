    Frequently Asked Questions About Residential Plumbing Service in Brandon

    Pricing depends on the job. A leaky faucet or running toilet usually runs $150 to $350, a water heater installation is often $1,200 to $3,500, and slab leak repairs or full repipes can reach several thousand dollars. The exact number comes after the diagnosis.

    Hard water carries dissolved calcium and magnesium. Soft water has had most of those minerals removed, usually by an ion-exchange softener, and the difference shows up as scale in water heaters, white crust on faucets, spotty dishes and soap that will not lather well. Soft water is easier on fixtures.

    Every one to two years works for most homes in Brandon, MS. The inspection usually runs about an hour, and we check pressure, look for slow leaks at shutoffs and supply lines, test the water heater relief valve and look for corrosion on exposed pipe. Small finds cost far less to fix.

    Emergencies go to the top of the board. We aim to have a plumber at the house within a few hours, and for a burst pipe or sewage backup we will walk you through closing the main shutoff on the phone so the damage stops while we drive. Keep towels ready.

    Soft water and normal pressure prevent a lot of what we see. Grease out of the drains and a yearly pressure test help too, since anything over 80 psi wears out supply hoses and fixtures faster than people expect. A yearly inspection catches the rest.

    Gurgling is the early one. After that come slow drains, a sewer smell and water rising in the shower pan, and when more than one fixture acts up at the same time, the clog is usually in the main line rather than a single trap. A main line clog only gets worse.

    Two leaks in a year is the tipping point for most homes. Polybutylene and galvanized pipe also tilt the answer toward a repipe, since patching one spot on a failing system just moves the next leak a few feet down the line. We price both side by side.

    Tightening a loose connection or plunging a toilet is fine. We usually advise against complex repairs on your own, because a bad sweat joint, a cross-threaded fitting or a gas connection done wrong can cost far more than the original repair would have. Gas work in particular should go to a licensed plumber.
