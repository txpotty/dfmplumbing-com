    From Tank to Drain Field: Septic Repair in Brandon, MS

    Camera Inspection of Septic Baffles in Brandon, MS

    Some tanks have no riser, so we run a push camera through the small inspection port to see the baffles. That saves digging up the whole lid. A cracked or missing outlet tee means we dig at the outlet end alone, then cut in a new PVC tee and an effluent filter housing. Concrete lids get a riser while they are open.

    Aerobic and Alternative System Repair in Brandon, MS

    Aerobic treatment units use an air pump to feed oxygen to the bacteria, and when that pump dies the unit starts to smell within days. Our techs rebuild linear aerators and swap rotary ones, plus clean out diffusers clogged with solids. Timers and panels get tested too. We also keep the service log your permit may require.

    Emergency Septic Repair in Brandon

    Sewage in the shower pan on a Sunday is an emergency, and we treat it like one. The first thing we ask is whether the tank has a riser we can reach, because digging for a buried lid adds an hour to the call. Stop using water until the tech arrives. Then we find the blockage, clear it and tell you what caused it.

    Inlet and Outlet Baffle Replacement in Brandon, MS

    Baffles hold back the scum layer on top and the sludge on the bottom so only clear effluent leaves the tank. Concrete ones get eaten by sulfuric acid from sewer gas and eventually fall off into the tank. Once the outlet baffle is gone, solids head straight for the field. We replace them with PVC sanitary tees. Usually a short job.

    Septic Pump and Alarm Repair in Brandon

    Pumped systems move effluent uphill or to a far field, and the pump sits in its own chamber with a float tree and an alarm. An alarm going off means the water rose past the high-level float. Our tech meters the pump and tests each float before replacing anything. Zoeller and Liberty parts ride on the truck.

    Drain Field Line Jetting in Brandon

    Distribution laterals clog from the inside with solids and slime, especially after years without pumping. A jetter goes into each lateral from the D-box or an end cleanout, and the water that comes back tells us how bad it is. Roots get cut. If the soil itself has sealed, jetting will not fix it, and we say so.

    Septic Tee Repair in Brandon

    Sanitary tees on the inlet and outlet crack or pop loose, sometimes from a careless pump-out hose. Without the outlet tee, the floating scum layer drifts into the laterals and plugs the gravel. We glue in a new Schedule 40 tee. An effluent filter goes on if the outlet has room.

    After-Hours Septic Backup Calls in Brandon

    Late-night backups usually start in the lowest drain in the house, often a basement floor drain or a first-floor shower. The after-hours truck has a jetter and spare floats on board, which means most calls end with the drains working. Bad nights end with a pump truck. Either way, you know what failed before we leave.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Septic Tank Repair in Brandon

    Small repairs like a broken tee or a stuck float usually run $300 to $700. An effluent pump with new floats typically costs $900 to $2,000, and field replacement can reach $5,000 to $15,000 or more depending on size and soil. You get the number after we diagnose it.

    Too little pumping is behind most early failures, because the sludge layer creeps up until it washes out the outlet. Roots and heavy trucks over the field speed it up. Large doses of bleach or chemical drain cleaner kill off the bacteria doing the work. Dense soil can add to it.

    Soil decides how fast the field can take water, which decides how long it lasts. Expansive clay soils are common in Mississippi, swelling with rain and shrinking in dry spells, which puts stress on foundations and buried pipes. Dense clay soaks water in slowly and backs up the trenches in wet weather, while very sandy or gravelly ground lets effluent through before it is cleaned. We dig a test hole before quoting field work.

    It depends on where you live, since sale inspections are set by state law and by the local health department in each area. Regular pumping and maintenance keep a septic system working, and slow drains in a rural home often point to the tank first. Even where it is optional, many buyers request one. We fix whatever the inspection turns up and give you paperwork the buyer can use.

    Our workmanship on every repair, plus the manufacturer's warranty on new pumps and alarms. You will find the full terms written on the invoice. Should a part we installed quit early, we handle the claim with the supplier so you do not have to chase it.

    We repair Zoeller and Liberty pumps nearly every week, along with SJE Rhombus alarm panels and the float switches that run them. We work on concrete tanks and plastic tanks alike. We also service aerobic treatment units, including their aerators and timer panels.

    A tee or pump swap usually needs no permit. Field work or a new tank usually requires one, and the health department typically inspects before anything is covered. Our office files it and our tech meets the inspector.
