    Trenchless Sewer Repair Options in Brandon, MS

    Where applicable, we offer trenchless sewer repair methods like pipe bursting. This technique involves pulling a new pipe through the old one, simultaneously breaking the existing pipe apart. It's a less disruptive option that requires only two small access points, saving your landscaping and driveway from extensive excavation. Trenchless methods are ideal for certain types of pipe damage and property layouts in Brandon.

    Addressing Tree Root Intrusions in Brandon, MS

    Tree roots are a common cause of sewer line damage, especially with older, more porous pipe materials like clay or cast iron. Roots can penetrate tiny cracks, grow inside the pipe, and eventually cause blockages or even burst the line. Our sewer replacement services specifically address root-damaged pipes, often recommending more resistant materials like PVC or ABS to stop future intrusions. We also advise on tree placement to protect your new sewer system.

    Clay and Orangeburg Pipe Replacement in Brandon, MS

    Clay pipe joints let roots in, while Orangeburg pipe made from tar paper deforms over time. Both are likely to fail. We replace these lines with modern materials. Camera inspection shows the full extent of damage. Replacement ends ongoing root problems.

    Sewer Pipe Material Upgrades in Brandon, MS

    Many older homes in Brandon, MS still have original sewer lines made of clay, cast iron, or Orangeburg pipe, which are prone to degradation, cracking, and root intrusion over time. When we perform a sewer replacement, we often recommend upgrading to modern, durable materials like PVC (polyvinyl chloride) or ABS (acrylonitrile butadiene styrene). These materials are more flexible, resistant to corrosion and roots, and have a much longer lifespan, providing a major upgrade to your home's plumbing infrastructure.

    Traditional Trench Sewer Replacement in Brandon

    For severely damaged or collapsed sewer lines, traditional trenching is often the most reliable solution. This involves excavating a trench along the sewer line's path to allow for direct access and replacement of the entire pipe. While more invasive, it provides a complete overhaul and is often required for older homes with clay or cast iron pipes that have reached the end of their lifespan. We ensure careful planning and execution to minimize disruption to your yard.
