    Inlet Screen and Air Filter Cleaning in Brandon

    Grit gets in. The small screen on the cold inlet catches sand and loose scale after a water main repair. On units with an air intake filter, dust and lint load it up and the burner runs rich or throws a combustion code. We clean both on every visit.

    Recirculation and Combi Unit Service in Brandon, MS

    Built-in recirculation pumps and buffer tanks get hot water to the tap faster, but they also push more water through the exchanger every day, so those units scale sooner. The recirc loop gets flushed with the exchanger. Then we check whether the system uses a crossover valve or a dedicated return line and set the schedule to match.

    Tankless Banks for Restaurants and Apartments in Brandon

    Cascade systems link several tankless units to one controller that fires them in turn. Heavy use means scale builds faster than in a single home unit. We descale each heater in the bank, check the cascade controller and confirm the load is shared evenly across every unit. Quarterly service is common.

    Home Tankless Flushing in Brandon

    Iron can leave rusty stains on fixtures and laundry, and sulfur odors often trace back to the water heater anode or the well itself. Inside a tankless heater, that water runs through a small copper or stainless coil while the burner fires, and every minute of hot water leaves a little calcium behind. We hook a pump to the service valves and circulate descaling solution for about an hour. The flow returns. Temperatures stop swinging, and the unit quits shutting down on high-limit.

    Tankless Vent Inspection in Brandon

    Gas tankless heaters push exhaust out through a vent that has to stay sealed from the heater all the way to the termination outside the house. Condensing units use PVC or polypropylene vent pipe, pitched back toward the heater so condensate drains. Every joint gets checked, and we test for carbon monoxide by the heater. Wasp nests in vent caps are common.

    Heat Exchanger Descaling in Brandon

    Picture a bundle of thin tubes with a gas flame under them and cold water racing through at three or four gallons a minute. That is your heat exchanger. Minerals settle on the hottest tube walls first, and the passages get narrower. We dissolve it with 45 to 60 minutes of pump-driven descaling solution.

    Navien Condensing Tune-Ups in Brandon

    Navien NPE units are condensing heaters with a second heat exchanger that pulls extra heat out of the exhaust before it leaves the house. That second exchanger makes acidic condensate. The condensate trap gets cleared and the neutralizer media checked. A-series models with built-in recirculation also get a pump and buffer tank check.
