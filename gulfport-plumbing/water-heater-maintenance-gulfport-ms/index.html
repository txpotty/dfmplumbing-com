    Tankless Water Heater Service in Gulfport, MS

    Tankless units need descaling. Mineral scale coats the inside of the heat exchanger, restricting flow and making the unit work harder, and eventually it starts throwing error codes or cutting out in the middle of a shower. We flush it with descaler. Igniter, fan and exhaust venting get checked too.

    Water Heater Leak Checks in Gulfport, MS

    Small drips become floods. We inspect the supply connections, the relief valve outlet, the drain valve and the tank seams for moisture or rust trails, and we check the drain pan and the floor around the unit for signs of a slow leak. Rust stains are a warning. Catching it early saves the floor.

    Gas Water Heater Safety Checks in Gulfport, MS

    Gas units need a clean burner and a good draft. Leak checks, flame inspection, burner cleaning and a draft test are part of every gas visit. Carbon monoxide is the concern. The thermocouple or flame sensor gets checked as well.

    Anode Rod Replacement in Gulfport

    Some water eats anode rods fast. Water chemistry can wear a rod out in a few years, so we check it every time. We swap worn ones. It keeps rust off the tank lining.

    Gas Burner and Venting Checks in Gulfport

    A blue flame is what we want to see. Yellow or lazy flames mean poor combustion, so we clean the burner and combustion chamber, check the thermocouple or flame sensor, and confirm the vent has a proper draft and tight connections. Bad venting is dangerous. The gas line and shutoff get leak tested.

    Tank Flush and Inspection in Gulfport

    Sediment forms from minerals in the water. On a standard tank visit we flush that sediment out, test the temperature and pressure relief valve, check the anode for corrosion and inspect every fitting for leaks, which protects one of the more expensive appliances in the house. The tank heats better. Recovery speeds up.

    Electric Element Service in Gulfport, MS

    Two elements, two thermostats. Sediment coats the lower element first, it runs hot and burns out, and that is the most common reason an electric tank suddenly gives you only half a tank of hot water. Power gets shut off first. Good parts stay in.
