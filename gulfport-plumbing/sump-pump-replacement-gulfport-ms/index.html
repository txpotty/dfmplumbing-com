    Pedestal Sump Pump Replacement in Gulfport, MS

    Pedestal pumps keep the motor up on a column above the pit, out of the water, which is why a good one can run fifteen years in a dry basement. You will hear one kick on from upstairs. We set the new column plumb, secure it so vibration does not walk it across the basin, and adjust the float rod stops for the cycle you want. They fit pits as narrow as 10 inches.

    Yard Drainage Pit Pumps in Gulfport, MS

    Yard drainage pits and window well pumps can sit dry from fall to spring and then seize the first time a big storm fills them. We swap in a corrosion-resistant pump and clear out leaves and silt. We route the outlet away from the house. Test them each spring.

    Float Switch and Check Valve Replacement in Gulfport

    Stuck floats and leaking check valves cause more sump failures than dead motors do. When the check valve leaks, the water in the pipe drops back into the basin and the pump has to move it twice, which shortens its life. New valve every time. In a tight pit we use a vertical switch, and in a wide pit a tethered float.

    Elevator and Garage Pit Pumps in Gulfport

    Elevator pits and parking garage sumps often require oil-sensing controls that stop the pump from sending hydraulic fluid or motor oil into the storm drain. We replace those pumps and controls to meet code. Two alternating pumps on one controller mean one can fail without flooding the pit. Some insurance carriers ask for test records.

    Backup Pump Options in Gulfport, MS

    After a flood, checking septic systems, well heads and water heaters is important before returning to normal use. Outages tend to hit right when the rain is heaviest and the pit is filling fastest. The backup kicks on if the primary dies or the power goes out. Water-powered backups use city water pressure and need no battery, but they only make sense with strong municipal pressure and an approved backflow preventer on the line. They will not work on a well.

    Sizing a Submersible Sump Pump in Gulfport

    Horsepower matters less than people think. A third-horse pump moves plenty of water for most homes, as long as the vertical lift to the discharge is under about 15 feet. Before we choose, we read the pump curve at your real discharge height. A pump that is too big empties the pit too fast and wears itself out.

    Choosing a Backup Battery in Gulfport

    Battery choice decides how long the backup actually runs. Most backups use a sealed AGM or deep-cycle battery around 75 to 100 amp-hours, which covers several hours of on-and-off pumping. We ask how often your pump runs in a storm and size the battery to match. Plan on a new battery every three to five years.

    Pedestal Pumps for Shallow Pits in Gulfport, MS

    Older basements sometimes have a narrow, shallow pit poured right into the floor, and a pedestal pump is often the only thing that fits without breaking concrete. We swap the pump, replace the float rod and stops, and run new 1.5-inch PVC to the check valve. They usually cost less than a submersible.
