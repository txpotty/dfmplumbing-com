    Jet Pump Replacement in Gulfport, MS

    Jet pumps sit above ground, in a well house or basement, and pull water up by suction from wells where the water level is within about 25 feet. Deep-well jets with a two-pipe setup can reach further, but they work harder. Lost prime is the classic complaint. Most of the time a bad foot valve or a suction leak is letting water drain back, and we check those before condemning the pump.

    Jet Pump Seal and Motor Replacement in Gulfport, MS

    Single-stage and two-stage jet pumps wear out at the seal and impeller, and a leaking shaft seal shows up as a puddle under the motor. New pump, primed and tested. After that, we watch the pressure gauge cycle from cut-in to cut-out.

    Emergency Well Pump Replacement in Gulfport

    Dry faucets on a well-only property mean no flushing or cooking, so we treat those as same-day calls whenever the crew and the weather allow. The truck carries common 1/2 and 3/4 HP pumps. We also bring splice kits and switches.

    Drop Pipe and Wire Replacement in Gulfport

    Galvanized drop pipe from the 1970s and 1980s rusts from the inside, and a pinhole at 150 feet will bleed pressure every time the pump shuts off. Depending on depth and pump weight, it gets replaced with schedule 120 PVC or poly. Wire gets the same look. Nicked jackets and old tape splices go.

    Submersible Well Pump Replacement in Gulfport

    A submersible pump hangs on drop pipe inside a 4-inch or 6-inch casing, often 100 to 400 feet down, so pulling one is a two-person job with a pump hoist. Sand in the impellers and lightning surges are the usual culprits. We megger the motor before we pull it. The reading shows whether the fault is the motor or the wire.

    Jet Pump Suction Line Repair in Gulfport, MS

    Foot valves and suction lines cause most jet pump trouble, not the pump. A pinhole in the suction pipe lets air in, the pump loses prime, and it runs dry until the seal burns up. The suction line gets a pressure test. A well that is too deep for suction needs a submersible, and we will quote that instead.
