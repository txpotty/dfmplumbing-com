    Frequently Asked Questions About Copper Pipe Leak Repair in Southaven

    Accessible copper leak repairs typically cost $250 to $650. Hidden pipe runs higher. Repairs inside finished walls or ceilings usually range from $500 to $1,500 before drywall patching, while slab work on copper tends to land between $1,500 and $4,000. We write it up before we start.

    We typically use Type L copper. It has a thicker wall. Type L lasts longer than the Type M found in many older homes, while press fittings or a PEX transition get used when a torch is a bad idea. All materials are potable-rated.

    Wall thickness. Type K is the heaviest. Type K is used for underground service lines, Type L is the standard for interior water lines in many codes, and Type M has the thinnest wall, which is why it tends to pinhole first in older homes. Our repairs use Type L.

    The pipe wall wears through from the inside. Water quality is the big factor. Aggressive water, high velocity, excess pressure, poor original soldering and stray electrical current from grounding issues each thin the wall until it pins through. Hot water speeds it up.

    Keep pressure in range. Test it yearly. Soften hard water, limit recirculation pump run time and get any green crust on fittings looked at early, while homes with older copper should have it checked every few years. Small fixes now save drywall later.

    Minor repairs typically do not. Larger work often does. Significant repiping, relocating lines or work on the main water service in Southaven may need a plumbing permit, depending on local rules and how much of the system is being changed. The paperwork is on us.

    An accessible pinhole or a single joint takes one to four hours. Hidden leaks add time. Leaks under a slab or a repipe can take a full day or more, depending on access, whether the line is rerouted and how much copper needs replacing. The quote includes a time estimate.

    A bill that climbs for no reason is a big one. Musty smells count. Damp spots on walls or ceilings, the sound of running water with every fixture off each suggest water is escaping out of sight. The meter will confirm it.
