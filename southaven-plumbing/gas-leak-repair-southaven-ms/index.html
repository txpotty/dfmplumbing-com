    What Our Gas Leak Repair Visits Cover in Southaven, MS

    Residential Gas Leak Detection in Southaven

    A gas leak in a house rarely sits where the smell is strongest, because gas drifts along joists and collects in closets and basements. We start with a gauge on the system to confirm there is a leak at all. Then the sniffer comes out. A combustible gas detector follows the reading from fitting to fitting until it spikes. Bubble solution on the suspect joint gives the final confirmation.

    Propane Gas Leak Repair in Southaven, MS

    Propane behaves differently from natural gas because it is heavier than air, so a leak settles into basements and crawlspaces instead of rising. The first-stage regulator at the tank and the second-stage regulator at the house both get checked. Low spots get sniffed first. Your propane supplier owns the tank in most cases, so we coordinate with them for anything on the tank side of the first regulator. Rural properties with underground tanks get a line test too.

    Appliance Gas Line Repair in Southaven, MS

    Most appliance leaks show up at the flex connector or the shutoff valve behind the appliance, usually right at a flare nut where the two meet. Connectors get bent every time a range is pulled out to clean behind it. Valves weep at the stem. We swap in a new coated stainless connector, add a quarter-turn ball valve and set a sediment trap where the code calls for it. Each joint gets a detector pass and a bubble test before the pilot is lit again.

    Commercial Gas Leak Repair in Southaven

    Commercial gas piping often runs at 2 psi or higher with regulators at each appliance bank, so a leak test there is not the same as a house test. We check regulator lockup and vent lines along with the joints. Rooftop units leak too. If a shutdown would cost you a dinner rush, we valve off just the bad branch and leave the rest live. Staff get a short briefing on the shutoff locations.

    Permitting and Code Compliance in Southaven, MS

    Most jurisdictions want a permit for new gas pipe or a moved line, though a straight connector swap often does not need one. You will know before we start whether a permit applies. The fee goes on the estimate. The work follows Mississippi adopted fuel gas code and any local amendments. The signed inspection card goes to you, and a future buyer's inspector will likely ask to see it.

    Gas Line Replacement and Upgrades in Southaven, MS

    Adding a gas range or a tankless heater can overload pipe that was sized years ago for just a furnace and a water heater. We add up the BTU load and measure the run length before choosing a pipe size. Undersized pipe starves burners. Where the old pipe is rusty or poorly threaded, we replace it with black iron or CSST, whichever reaches easier. A big tankless unit may also need a larger meter from the utility.

    Red Tag Gas Repairs in Southaven

    A red tag on your meter or appliance means the utility found a leak or unsafe condition and will not restore gas until a licensed contractor fixes it. The tag usually says what they found, so we start there. Then we test everything. Once the repair is done, we perform the required test and send the documentation to the utility or the inspector. Relights are scheduled with the utility, usually within a day or two.

    Appliance Connector Replacement in Southaven

    Old uncoated brass connectors, the kind made before the mid-1970s, are the subject of consumer safety warnings because the flare nut can separate from the tube. If your range or water heater still has one, it should come out. We see them weekly. The replacement is a coated stainless connector sized for the appliance, with a new ball valve and a sediment trap where one is required. Bubble testing follows before the pilot is lit.
