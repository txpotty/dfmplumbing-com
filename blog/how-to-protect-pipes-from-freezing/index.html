    How to Protect Your Pipes Before the Next Mississippi Freeze

    By Andrew Blake · October 6, 2026 · 5 min read

    Where Mississippi Homes Freeze First

    Pipes do not freeze evenly through a house. They freeze where cold air reaches them and heat does not, like an exterior wall behind a kitchen sink or a crawlspace with an open vent. Hose bibs are the classic failure. The water inside is only inches from the outdoors.

    Freezing weather is infrequent in Mississippi, but a hard freeze every few winters can catch homes that were never built for it.

    Ice rarely splits the pipe at the frozen spot. The pressure builds between the ice plug and a closed faucet, and the pipe bursts in a section that looks fine. A split half-inch copper line can push out several gallons a minute once it thaws. The crack often sits inside a wall, quiet, until the thaw.

    Why a Frozen Line Does So Much Damage

    A burst supply line keeps running at full house pressure, usually somewhere between 50 and 80 psi, until somebody shuts the main valve. Overnight, that can mean hundreds of gallons in a wall cavity. Drywall and insulation soak it up first. Mold can start on wet drywall within two days if it is not dried out.

    Steps That Keep Pipes From Freezing

    Start outside, since that is where most freeze calls begin. Pull every garden hose off its spigot before the first hard frost. A hose left on a frost-free sillcock traps water in the valve body and defeats the whole point of the valve. Older spigots without the frost-free stem need an inside shutoff closed and the outside valve left open to drain.

    • Slip foam sleeves over exposed lines in crawlspaces and garages.
    • Seal gaps where pipes pass through exterior walls with spray foam or caulk.
    • Close crawlspace vents for the winter, and open them again in spring.
    • Open sink cabinet doors on outside walls during a hard freeze.
    • Keep the thermostat at 55 or higher, even when you are away.

    On the coldest nights, let a faucet on an outside wall drip. A slow drip, about the width of a pencil lead, keeps water moving and relieves pressure if ice does form. Drip both hot and cold. Single-handle faucets should sit in the middle.

    Heat tape is worth the money on any line you already know freezes every single winter, like a crawlspace run under a bathroom. Use a UL-listed self-regulating cable instead of the bargain constant-wattage type. Plug it into a GFCI outlet and check it each fall before the cold arrives. Never wrap tape over itself.

    If Your Pipes Freeze Anyway

    Turn on the faucet the frozen line feeds. A trickle or nothing at all tells you where the plug is. Warm the pipe with a hair dryer or space heater, starting at the faucet end and working back toward the ice. Never use a torch. Open flame inside a wall has burned down more houses than frozen pipes have flooded.

    Know where your main shutoff is before you need it. Look where the water line comes into the house, usually the garage or crawlspace, or check the meter box by the street. Turn it off the moment you see water. Our emergency line at DFM Plumbing is (601) 265-9260, and a tech can usually be on the way within the hour.

    Long-Term Fixes for Lines That Keep Freezing

    A pipe that freezes twice will freeze again. The lasting fix is to move it or insulate the cavity around it. During a remodel we can shift a supply line from an outside wall to an inside wall, and that ends the problem permanently. PEX also handles a freeze better than copper, since it can swell a little without splitting. It is not freeze-proof, though.

    Our plumbers winterize homes all over Mississippi and can check a crawlspace or attic for exposed lines in about an hour. A short inspection before winter costs far less than drying out a ceiling. Vacant homes need extra care. Frost-free sillcocks and new shutoff valves are common add-ons on the same visit.
