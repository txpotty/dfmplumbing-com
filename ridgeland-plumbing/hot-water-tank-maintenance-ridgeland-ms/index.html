    Sediment Flush and Descaling in Ridgeland

    Hard water leaves calcium carbonate behind every time the burner heats the tank. Over a year that layer can harden into chunks that insulate the water from the flame and cause loud popping. We connect a hose to the drain valve, flush with full line pressure and agitate the bottom until the water runs clear. When scale is severe we use a descaling solution approved for potable systems. A clean tank heats faster and puts less stress on the bottom seam.

    Electric Element and Thermostat Service in Ridgeland

    Electric tanks rely on upper and lower heating elements that get crusted with scale in hard water. We test each element with a meter, check the thermostats and look for scorched wires at the terminal block. If an element has failed we replace it with the matching wattage and voltage. Plenty of families who think they need a new heater just need a lower element and a flush.

    Relief Valve and Expansion Tank Testing in Ridgeland

    The temperature and pressure relief valve is the part that keeps a tank from becoming dangerous. We also check your thermal expansion tank pressure with a gauge, since a waterlogged expansion tank lets pressure spike every heating cycle. High street pressure is common in many service areas, so we test static pressure at a hose bib too.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Hot Water Tank Maintenance in Ridgeland

    The cost for hot water tank maintenance in Ridgeland, MS usually ranges from $100 to $250, depending on the tank type, age and specific services required like anode rod replacement. We provide a detailed quote before any work begins.

    Most manufacturers and plumbing experts recommend annual hot water tank maintenance. This is especially true where water is hard, since minerals speed up sediment buildup and anode rod depletion.

    Neglecting maintenance can lead to inefficient operation, higher energy bills, premature tank failure from corrosion or sediment buildup, and potential safety hazards from a malfunctioning pressure relief valve. Regular service heads off these costly and dangerous issues.

    That sound is water trapped under a layer of sediment boiling as the burner fires. A flush usually quiets it, though very old hardened scale may not come out completely.

    The anode rod, also known as a sacrificial rod, is a metal rod inside your hot water tank designed to corrode before the tank itself. It protects the tank lining from rust and corrosion, which matters most when water carries a lot of minerals, and it extends the lifespan of your unit.

    While some basic steps like draining a small amount of water can be done by a homeowner, professional hot water tank maintenance involves specialized tools and experience to properly inspect electrical or gas components, anode rods and pressure relief valves. It is best left to a professional to protect safety and code compliance.

    That smell typically comes from bacteria reacting with a magnesium anode. We can switch to an aluminum-zinc or powered anode and sanitize the tank to clear it up.

    Many homeowners can do a basic drain with a garden hose. The risk is a plastic drain valve that breaks or will not reseal, and DIY flushes rarely include an anode check or relief valve test.
