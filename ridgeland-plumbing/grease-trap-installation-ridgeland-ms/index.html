    Hydro-Mechanical Grease Trap Installation in Ridgeland, MS

    Hydromechanical traps use baffles and a vented flow control to pull grease out of water moving at a set rate, usually 20 to 100 gallons per minute. They fit indoors, under a sink or in a floor pit. The flow control is not optional. Without it, a dumped sink pushes water through faster than the unit is rated for and the grease goes out the outlet. We look for units certified to PDI G-101 or ASME A112.14.3.

    Commercial Grease Trap Installation in Ridgeland

    A commercial kitchen with a three-compartment sink, a pre-rinse station and two floor sinks can push 50 gallons a minute of greasy water at the dinner rush. Our sizing starts from your peak flow, the worst ten minutes of the night, rather than a daily average. Undersized traps are the usual failure. We set the unit, run the inlet and outlet in schedule 40 PVC or cast iron, and add the flow control fitting the manufacturer calls for.

    Grease Trap Installation Cost in Ridgeland, MS

    New Restaurant Build-Out Grease Traps in Ridgeland

    On a new build-out, the grease trap gets decided before the slab is poured, not after. The drawings and the equipment list from your kitchen designer tell us what will drain where.     Hydromechanical Grease Trap Installation in Ridgeland

    Flow rating is the number that matters on a hydromechanical trap, and it has to match the fastest-draining fixture combination in your kitchen. Put a 50 GPM unit on a 75 GPM load and grease goes out the back at every rush. We do the math on paper. Each sink compartment's volume, the drain size and a one or two minute drain time give us the GPM figure the code uses.

    Gravity Grease Trap Installation in Ridgeland

    A gravity interceptor works on time, not machinery. The tank holds water for roughly half an hour, which gives grease time to rise and food solids time to drop. Sizing comes from your fixture units and the retention time your sewer authority wants, and we check that number with them before ordering a tank. No moving parts to fail.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Ridgeland

    Small under-sink hydromechanical traps usually land between $1,000 and $3,000 installed, and buried interceptors with excavation commonly run $5,000 to $20,000 or more. Most of the difference is labor, mainly digging and concrete repair. Permits add a little. You get a written, itemized number after we see the kitchen.

    Half a day for most indoor units. That covers setting the trap, piping the inlet and outlet, installing the flow control and running a water test. Gravity interceptors take two to five days on site, plus time for the inspector to see the tank before backfill and again at final. Permits add lead time.

    Coverage comes from two places. Your trap carries a factory warranty that varies by brand and material. Our written labor warranty covers the joints we made and how we set the unit. Damage from skipped cleanings falls outside it.

    Pump-outs every couple of weeks on a trap that used to go two months is the clearest sign you have outgrown it or the baffles have failed. So is a sour grease smell near the floor sinks. Rust shows up too. We also look downstream, because grease in the outlet pipe means the trap is letting it through.

    Nearly every food business needs one. Plumbing codes require a grease interceptor wherever fixtures discharge grease-laden waste, which covers restaurants, bakeries and most food prep spaces. Some coffee shops and prepackaged food counters get a waiver, but that decision belongs to the local pretreatment office, not the landlord or the previous tenant. Ask before you sign the lease.

    Plastic hydromechanical traps often last 20 years or more, while coated steel traps can rust out in 7 to 15 depending on how harsh the wastewater runs. Concrete tanks can last decades, though hydrogen sulfide gas slowly eats the concrete above the water line. Cleaning habits matter most. Rough cleaning and long gaps between pump-outs shorten the life of every type.

    Two warranties apply here. Manufacturer coverage on the unit varies by brand and material, and plastic bodies usually get the longer terms. We cover our workmanship, meaning leaks at our joints and settling of a tank we set, and we give you the terms in writing before we start.

    Plan on a permit. Grease interceptors are regulated by both the plumbing code and the local sewer use ordinance, so two offices may want to see the plans. We file both when needed and give you copies of the approvals, which your health inspector may ask to see at opening. Unpermitted traps cause trouble at lease transfer.
