    Venting and Draft Repair in Ridgeland, MS

    Atmospheric tanks rely on hot exhaust rising through a metal flue with an upward slope of at least a quarter inch per foot. Sections that pull apart, rust through or sag let combustion gas spill into the room. The vent gets repaired. Each joint gets three screws, the connector gets proper clearance from wood and drywall, and we run the heater for several minutes with a smoke test at the draft hood to watch the exhaust go up. Spillage gets fixed before we leave.

    Burner Assembly and Venting Repair in Ridgeland

    Rust scale on the burner tray is a sign the flue baffle or the tank top is shedding. We pull the burner, clear the ports and look at the flame arrestor screen underneath. Then the vent gets checked from the draft hood to the roof, because a flue that is loose, sagging or capped by a bird nest can push carbon monoxide back into your Ridgeland home. We hold a smoke pencil at the draft hood to see which way the air moves.

    Bradford White and Rheem Gas Water Heater Repair in Ridgeland, MS

    Bradford White and Rheem tanks make up a big share of what we repair in Ridgeland, MS. Most Rheem gas units run a White-Rodgers or Honeywell valve with a blinking status LED. Bradford White uses its own sealed combustion design with a lint screen at the base and a thermal switch, and it needs a specific kit when the pilot assembly goes bad. Both kits ride on the truck. Using factory parts keeps your tank warranty valid.

    Gas Control Valve Replacement in Ridgeland, MS

    Honeywell and White-Rodgers valves report faults with a blinking LED, and the flash count matches a chart on the label. The tech reads the code and tests the thermopile and sensors before condemning the valve. Valves do fail. When the control itself is the problem, we replace it with an exact-match valve or a listed universal model, set the temperature and leak test every joint on the gas train before relighting. You get the old valve back if you want it.

    Burner Cleaning and Repair in Ridgeland, MS

    Yellow flame tips, soot on the burner door or a roaring sound at startup all mean the burner is not getting clean air. The burner comes out, the ports get brushed and the orifice gets cleared. We also confirm the orifice is drilled for the fuel you have, because a natural gas orifice on a propane heater, or the reverse, will soot up the flue and push carbon monoxide levels well past normal. A good flame is blue and steady. Rust flakes on the burner tell us to look up the flue.

    Pilot Light and Ignition Repair in Ridgeland

    No hot water and a dark pilot window usually point to the ignition side of the heater. The tech pulls the pilot assembly and checks the thermocouple tip, the orifice and the igniter wire. A thermocouple that tests weak on the millivolt meter will not hold the gas valve's magnet open, so the pilot drops out a minute after you relight it by the label. Most trucks carry the common pilot kits.

    Propane Water Heater Repair in Ridgeland

    Propane and natural gas heaters look the same but burn at different pressures. Propane units need a matching orifice and a control set near 11 inches of water column. Natural gas runs closer to 7 inches, so a heater converted badly, or a regulator that drifted, gives you soot, a yellow flame and a pilot that keeps going out for no obvious reason. We check pressure with a manometer. A bad conversion gets corrected with the right parts.

    Gas Valve and Thermostat Repair in Ridgeland, MS

    Lukewarm showers and a tank that never catches up often trace back to the gas control. We check the thermostat setting, the immersion sensor and the valve's outlet pressure with a manometer. On most residential tanks the thermostat lives inside the gas valve body, so a bad sensor or a stuck magnet usually means replacing the whole control rather than one small part. Plan on roughly an hour for the swap. The new valve gets a leak test at every joint.
