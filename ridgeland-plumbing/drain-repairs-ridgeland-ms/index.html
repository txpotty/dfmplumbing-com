    Cast Iron Drain Stack Repair in Ridgeland, MS

    Old cast iron stacks rot from the inside out. Cracks start along the bottom.     Leaking Drain Pipe Repair in Ridgeland, MS

    Drain leaks are low pressure, so they drip instead of spray. Most go unnoticed for months. Corrosion, shifting framing and cracked fittings are the usual causes, and we trace the leak by running each fixture and watching the joints, then replace the bad length with pipe that matches what is already there. Repairs meet local plumbing code.

    Recurring Clog Repair in Ridgeland

    Monthly clogs mean there is a physical cause. Cleaning will not stop it. The usual suspects are a trap arm with too little slope, a branch line with a belly, scale built up inside old metal pipe or a fitting with a rough edge that snags paper, and each one shows up clearly on a camera or when we pull the trap. The fix goes where the clog starts.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Drain Repairs in Ridgeland

    Trap and slip-joint repairs typically cost $150 to $350. Replacing pipe costs more. A section of drain pipe usually runs $400 to $1,200, and cast iron stack replacement can range from $2,000 to $6,000 with drywall, flooring and access driving most of the spread. We write it up before we start.

    Keep grease out of the kitchen sink. Use hair screens. Flush only toilet paper, run hot water after greasy dishes, and have the main branches jetted every couple of years on older homes. Scale-prone lines benefit from yearly cleaning.

    We install PVC and ABS for most new drain work. Some jobs call for cast iron. Cast iron still makes sense in some multi-story walls where noise matters, and we tie new pipe into existing metal drains with listed transition fittings. All materials are code-listed.

    Hydro-jetting scours the pipe with water at several thousand psi. It cleans wall to wall. A snake only punches a hole through a clog, while a jetter removes grease, scale and fine roots from the full circumference of the pipe. We camera first on old or brittle pipe.

    PVC, ABS, cast iron, galvanized and copper drain all show up in Mississippi homes. We work on all of them. Joining new plastic to old metal takes a shielded transition coupling sized to the outside diameter of each pipe, because a mismatched coupling is the most common reason a drain repair drips. The common sizes ride on the truck.

    Repeat clogs come from something in the pipe catching debris. Usually it is buildup. Poor pipe slope, a missing vent or a cracked fitting with a rough edge will snag paper and hair until the line closes off again a few weeks after each cleaning. A camera shows which one you have.

    Every repair is warrantied. Workmanship is covered in writing. Replacement parts carry their manufacturer warranties, so you know the coverage on both parts and labor before you approve anything. The terms are plain.
