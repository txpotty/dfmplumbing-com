    Repiping for Hard Water in Ridgeland, MS

    Much of the water in Mississippi comes from underground aquifers and is fairly soft, though some supplies carry iron or a sulfur smell. Hard water leaves calcium and magnesium scale inside pipe, and over years it narrows the bore and speeds up corrosion in metal lines. Pressure suffers. In hard water homes, PEX-A is often the better pick since it resists scale, and a softener loop or filter can be added during the job. Planning it now saves a second visit later.

    Whole-Home Copper Repiping in Ridgeland

    Copper is the traditional choice, and Type L copper has a thicker wall than the Type M that went into many older homes. Type L tolerates UV, stays rigid and has decades of field history. It costs more. Our plumbers solder joints with lead-free solder or use press fittings, support the pipe at the spacing the code calls for and pressure test the system before any wall is closed.

    PEX Pipe Replacement in Ridgeland, MS

    PEX tubing resists freezing damage, corrosion and scale better than most metal pipe. It is quieter too. Flexible PEX soaks up some of the pressure spike behind water hammer, and its long fitting-free runs leave fewer weak points. We keep PEX out of sunlight and away from water heater flues.

    Residential Repiping Plans in Ridgeland

    Every house is piped a little differently, so a repipe starts with a walk-through. Our estimator counts fixtures, checks access, finds the main and asks about any planned remodels before choosing a route. Then we size the lines. A three-bath house with a tankless heater may need 1-inch main trunk lines to keep showers steady when the washer kicks on.

    Kitec and Polybutylene Replacement in Ridgeland

    Kitec, an orange or blue PEX-AL-PEX pipe with brass fittings, and gray polybutylene are both known for failures. Both still turn up in older homes. The pipe at the water heater usually tells us which one you have, and we replace the full system so the home is easier to insure and to sell.

    PEX Versus Copper Repipe Pricing in Ridgeland, MS

    PEX repipes usually cost less than copper because the material is cheaper and installation goes faster. Copper costs more. Copper commonly costs 20 to 40 percent more than PEX, and some owners prefer it anyway for its long history. Both prices include permits, fittings, patching and cleanup.
