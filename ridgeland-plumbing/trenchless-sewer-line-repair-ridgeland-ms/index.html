    Pipe Bursting Sewer Line Replacement in Ridgeland

    A bursting head is a steel cone, slightly wider than the old pipe, pulled through the line by a hydraulic unit or a winch cable. It cracks the old pipe outward into the soil while a string of new HDPE pipe follows right behind it. Fused joints, no gaps. We use it when the old line is too broken or flattened to hold a liner.

    Cast Iron Sewer Line Repair in Ridgeland, MS

    Cast iron rusts from the inside out, and the bottom of the pipe goes first. Plumbers call that channel rot, a groove that eats through the bottom. We descale the pipe with chain knockers or carbide cutters spinning on a flexible shaft, which brings the wall back to bare metal so a liner can bond to it. Under a slab, lining beats breaking up the floor in two rooms.

    Cured-In-Place Pipe Lining in Ridgeland, MS

    CIPP stands for cured-in-place pipe. We wet out a felt tube with epoxy resin, invert it into the cleaned line with air pressure, and hold it against the old pipe wall until the resin hardens. Ambient cure takes a few hours, and hot water or steam shortens it. The finished liner seals cracks and leaking joints in the original pipe.

    Sewer Line Video Inspection in Ridgeland

    We record the full camera run on every job, from the cleanout out to the city main or the septic tank. You get a copy of the video file and a written summary. On the footage you can see what we see, roots at a joint at 22 feet and an offset at 38 feet where the pipe dropped. Those footage marks tell us where to line and where a point repair will do.

    Trenchless vs Traditional Sewer Repair Cost in Ridgeland, MS

    Point repairs often cost $1,500 to $3,500, while full lining of a residential lateral often runs $4,000 to $12,000 depending on length and diameter. Pipe bursting typically ranges from $5,000 to $15,000. Open digging can be cheaper on short, shallow runs. Once you add back concrete and sod, the gap usually closes, and we price both options side by side so you can see the difference.

    Cured-In-Place Pipe Prep and Lining in Ridgeland

    Prep is most of the job. We jet the line to remove roots and grease, then camera it again to measure the exact footage before cutting the liner tube to length. A poor prep job means a wrinkled liner. Lining works best on cracked or root-intruded lines that still hold their round shape.

    Root Intrusion Repair in Ridgeland, MS

    Roots enter at the joints, where clay bells or cast iron hubs have a small gap or a failed gasket. Once inside, they branch into a mat that fills the pipe. We cut them out with a mechanical root cutter or a hydro jet, then line the pipe so those joints are sealed for good and the line stops needing a cleaning every few months. With no joints left, there is nowhere for them to return.

    Sectional Point Repair in Ridgeland

    A point repair is a short liner, often 3 to 5 feet long, set over one bad spot. It rides in on an inflatable packer, guided by the camera footage, and the packer inflates to press the resin-soaked sleeve against the pipe wall until it cures. It fixes a single crack or root entry, not a whole failing line. Sound lines with one bad joint are the right candidates.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Trenchless Sewer Line Repair in Ridgeland

    Most trenchless sewer jobs in Ridgeland, MS fall between $4,000 and $15,000. The biggest factors are the length of the run and whether we line or burst. Depth, pipe diameter and how hard the pits are to reach also move the number, so a line under a driveway with only one cleanout will cost more than an open-lawn lateral of the same length.

    Decades. Quality liners and HDPE pipe carry a 50-year design life from most manufacturers when the host pipe was cleaned and prepped right.

    Perma-Liner and MaxLiner are two of the systems we install. Both are established names in lining. The choice comes down to the pipe diameter, the material and how many bends the line has, since some liners handle 45- and 90-degree turns better than others do.

    Fifty years is the common design rating for cured-in-place liners and HDPE pipe. Material warranties often run past 25 years. How well the pipe was cleaned and prepped decides whether a liner reaches that number, so we record the prep on camera and keep the footage on file with your warranty.

    One to three days covers most jobs, while an open-trench replacement can run a week or longer once restoration is counted. Lining a short lateral can finish in a single morning. Longer runs and bursts take more time, mostly for pit work and fusing the new HDPE pipe into one length before the pull. Cure time adds a few hours.

    Plan on a day for most lining jobs and one to two days for a burst. A dig can take a week. You only lose use of the drains while the resin cures, which is usually a few hours, so most families never leave the house and can use the toilets again by dinner.

    Pipe bursting uses high-density polyethylene, HDPE, fused into one continuous length with no joints. Liners are felt tubes soaked in epoxy. Neither material corrodes, and roots cannot get through, and both are rated by their makers for a service life of around fifty years when installed on properly prepared pipe.
