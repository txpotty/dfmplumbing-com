    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbers for Frozen Pipes in Horn Lake

    Costs range from about $200 for simple thawing to $1,500 or more for a burst pipe inside a finished wall. Where the pipe sits matters most. A frozen line in an open crawlspace is quick work, while a split pipe in a second-floor ceiling means opening drywall, repairing the pipe and leaving the opening ready for a drywall patch.

    Most homes have water back within one to two hours of our arrival. A split pipe adds time. We isolate a split branch when we can, so the rest of the house has water while we repair it.

    A short, exposed section usually thaws in 30 to 60 minutes. Pipes inside walls take more time. A frozen line inside an exterior wall or under a floor can take a few hours, especially if it is PEX or CPVC, which we thaw with gentler infrared or warm air instead of an electric thawing machine.

    A faucet that trickles or runs dry after a cold night is the most common sign. Visible frost on a pipe confirms it. You may also notice a toilet that will not refill, a bulge in a pipe, or a strange smell from a drain, and any of those means you should open the faucet and call before the line thaws on its own.

    You can open the faucet and gently warm an exposed pipe with a hair dryer. Open flame is never safe here. Hidden pipes are better left to us, since a hidden split can flood the wall as it thaws.

    Repairs carry a written workmanship warranty, with the term on your invoice. Parts carry the maker's warranty. It covers the repair, not a future freeze, so we recommend protection for exposed lines.

    Wrapping exposed lines and letting faucets drip on the coldest nights avoid most of the damage a sudden freeze can cause.
