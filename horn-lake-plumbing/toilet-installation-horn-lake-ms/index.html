    Types of Toilet Installation in Horn Lake, MS

    High-Efficiency Toilet Installation in Horn Lake, MS

    High-efficiency toilets flush on 1.28 gallons or less, against 3.5 gallons or more for toilets from before the early 1990s. Check for the WaterSense label. We steer customers toward models with a MaP score of 800 grams or higher, because a cheap low-flow bowl that needs two flushes saves nothing. Dual-flush models add a lighter half flush.

    Two-Piece Toilet Installation in Horn Lake

    Two-piece toilets come with the tank and bowl separate, joined by a rubber gasket and two or three bolts. We assemble them on the floor before setting. Tightening the tank bolts evenly, side to side, matters more than how hard they go, because an uneven pull cracks the china or leaves a slow drip at one bolt. It should not wobble.

    Rough-In Toilet Installation in Horn Lake, MS

    Rough-in is the plumbing that goes in before walls and floors close up. For a toilet that means a 3 inch drain with a vent, plus a cold supply stub. We set the drain 12 inches from the framed wall plus the thickness of drywall, so the finished rough-in lands right where the fixture expects it. Then the inspector sees it open.

    Commercial Toilet Installation in Horn Lake, MS

    Commercial restrooms take a beating, so we install vitreous china bowls with flushometer valves rather than tank toilets. Flushometers want a 1 inch supply line and steady pressure. Before setting fixtures in an office or shop, we check that the building supply can deliver the flow the valve is rated for, often 20 to 25 gallons per minute at the fixture. Fixture counts follow code.

    ADA-Compliant Toilet Installation in Horn Lake

    ADA toilets put the seat 17 to 19 inches off the floor, and the centerline sits 16 to 18 inches from the side wall. That spacing leaves room for a grab bar. We measure the existing drain before ordering, since moving a drain two inches to hit the ADA centerline means opening the floor. Elongated bowls are standard here.

    Customer-Supplied Toilet Installation in Horn Lake, MS

    Bought the toilet yourself? Check the rough-in printed on the box first. We unbox it and look over the porcelain for shipping cracks before we pull your old one, since a hairline crack in the bowl or tank means it needs to go back to the store, not onto your floor. We bring the seal and stainless bolts.

    Low-Flow WaterSense Toilet Installation in Horn Lake, MS

    WaterSense toilets are capped at 1.28 gallons per flush, and some dual-flush models drop to around 0.8 for liquid waste. It adds up quickly. A family of four swapping one old 3.5 gallon toilet can save thousands of gallons a year, and some water providers offer rebates worth checking. Pick a strong MaP rating.

Most of the remaining time goes into cleaning the flange and setting the new bowl on shims so it does not rock on a floor that has settled over the years. We haul the old one away.
