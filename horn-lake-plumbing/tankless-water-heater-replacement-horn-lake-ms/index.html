    Tank to Tankless Conversions in Horn Lake, MS

    Converting from a 40 or 50 gallon tank to tankless frees up floor space and ends standby heat loss in a garage or closet. The catch is that tankless units often need a larger gas line, often three quarter inch or bigger, plus new venting. We evaluate your meter size and pipe run before quoting so there are no surprises mid-job. We also cap or reroute the old flue and seal the roof penetration if it is no longer used. Plan for a conversion to take a full day.

    Commercial Tankless Replacement in Horn Lake

    Restaurants, salons and small offices often use multiple tankless units linked together on a rack system. When one or more units fail, we replace them and rebalance the cascade controls so they split the load.     Condensing Tankless Replacement in Horn Lake

    Condensing tankless units like the Navien NPE series or Rinnai Sensei reach efficiencies above 90 percent by capturing heat from exhaust gases. They vent through inexpensive PVC and produce acidic condensate that must be neutralized and drained properly. We install a neutralizer cartridge and route the condensate line to an approved drain. Constant moisture speeds up corrosion on metal pipes and fittings and encourages mold around slow leaks. Condensing units still save gas because they work year-round on showers, laundry and dishes. We explain the cost difference so you can decide if the efficiency gain is worth the price.

    Exterior Mount Tankless Units in Horn Lake, MS

    Many homes have tankless heaters mounted on an exterior wall with no venting at all, since the unit exhausts directly outside. These units face sun, rain and temperature swings, which can break down plastic covers and wiring over time. Wrapping exposed lines and letting faucets drip on the coldest nights prevent most of the damage a sudden freeze can cause. When we replace them, we check the pipe cover, freeze protection settings and the electrical connection. We can add a protective pipe cover kit to guard the water and gas lines. Exterior models are often the easiest swap when the gas line is already sized correctly.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Tankless Water Heater Replacement in Horn Lake

    The cost for tankless water heater replacement in Horn Lake, MS usually ranges from $3,000 to $6,000, depending on the unit's brand, capacity, fuel type (gas or electric) and any needed changes to gas lines, electrical or venting. This estimate includes both the new unit and professional installation. We will provide a detailed quote upfront after assessing your needs.

    We install Navien, Rinnai, Noritz, Takagi and Rheem tankless heaters. Each has its own strengths, and we recommend based on your flow needs, venting options and budget.

    Frequent error codes, fluctuating temperature, water leaking from inside the cabinet and a unit older than 15 years are common signs. If the heat exchanger is leaking, replacement is typically the better value.

    A standard tankless water heater replacement, if you are upgrading from an existing tankless unit, typically takes 4 to 8 hours. If you are converting from a traditional tank water heater, the process can take longer, possibly a full day, due to extra plumbing, gas or electrical changes required for proper installation and venting.

    Tankless water heater replacement is a complex job that involves gas lines, electrical wiring, water connections and venting. It is highly recommended to hire a licensed professional like DFM Plumbing due to the safety risks and potential for improper installation, which can lead to leaks, gas hazards or inefficient operation. Professionals also protect code compliance and warranty validity.

    To prepare for tankless water heater replacement, make sure the area around your existing unit is clear and accessible for our plumbers. If possible, clear a path to the installation site and keep pets or children away from the work area. We will handle draining the old unit and all other technical preparations.

    Most manufacturers offer 10 to 15 years on the heat exchanger and about 5 years on parts for residential use. We add our own labor warranty and register the unit on your behalf.

    DIY tankless water heater replacement is usually not recommended due to the complexity of gas lines, high-voltage electricity, water connections and venting requirements.
