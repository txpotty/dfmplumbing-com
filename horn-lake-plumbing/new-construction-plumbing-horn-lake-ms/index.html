    Frequently Asked Questions About New Construction Plumbing in Horn Lake

    Most new single-family homes in the Horn Lake area cost between $12,000 and $25,000 to plumb, with larger custom homes running higher. Each added bathroom adds several thousand dollars. Tankless heaters and gas runs add to it. Our bid is fixed per phase, so the number you sign is the number you pay unless the plans change.

    Yes, we take single-phase jobs. We can handle only the underground, only top-out, or only trim. When someone else started the house, we test their work before connecting to it.

    Yes, our workmanship warranty carries past move-in for the term in your contract. Any leak at one of our fittings is on us. Damage from a nail through a pipe after we leave or a frozen hose bib with a hose left on is not a workmanship defect and is billed as a service call.

    Slab and crawlspace foundations are typical, and soil movement under a slab can crack drain lines and supply pipes. Soil movement works on any pipe buried under concrete. Our crews bed drains in sand or gravel, sleeve pipes through the slab and run PEX water services where permitted. Sleeves are cheap.

    PEX handles most water supply in new homes. It flexes and needs fewer fittings in the walls than copper. For drain, waste and vent we use Schedule 40 PVC or ABS, whichever your code and plans call for. Copper still shows up at water heaters.

    For supply, we use PEX-A or PEX-B in most homes, sometimes with a copper main. Drains are PVC or ABS. Some builders want cast iron in walls beside bedrooms, since it is far quieter than PVC when an upstairs toilet flushes.

    Most jurisdictions inspect plumbing three times. The underground inspection happens before the slab is poured, with drains under a water or air test. Top-out gets inspected before insulation, and the final comes after fixtures are set and running. Some add a gas test.

    Underground plumbing on a typical house takes one to three days. Top-out takes three to five days, and final trim takes one or two. The calendar time is longer, because each phase waits on concrete, framing, inspection or cabinets, so the plumber is on and off site over three to six months. Weather moves it too.
