    Supply Line Leak Repair in Meridian

    Water lines along a basement ceiling can leak at fittings, valves or pinholes. Bad sections get cut out and replaced, and a run with repeated pinholes gets repiped rather than patched. Repiping a basement ceiling is usually a one-day job.

    Floor Drain and Backwater Valve Repair in Meridian

    A floor drain that backs up when the washer drains or during a storm usually points to a blockage downstream or a failed backwater valve. After clearing the line, we run a camera to confirm it is open. In homes where the street sewer backs up in storms, a backwater valve stops sewage from rising through the floor drain. Dry traps smell.

    Clogged Drain Tile Repair in Meridian

    Footing drains clog with silt and roots over the years, and once they stop flowing the water table at the wall rises. We camera the tile from the sump inlet to see where it is blocked. Jetting clears it. Crushed clay tile has to be dug up and replaced with perforated PVC or corrugated pipe in a sock.

    Interior Drainage Systems in Meridian

    Where digging outside is not practical, an interior drain system does the same job from inside. We cut the slab along the perimeter, lay perforated pipe in washed stone, and slope it to a new or existing sump pit before patching the floor with fresh concrete. The wall gets a dimpled membrane. Seepage runs down behind it and into the pipe.

    Exterior Membrane Waterproofing in Meridian, MS

    Excavating the outside of a foundation puts the repair where the water actually gets in. Constant moisture accelerates corrosion on metal pipes and fittings and encourages mold around slow leaks. The membrane is matched to those conditions, the footing drain is replaced, and the backfill is graded to slope away. Downspouts get extended too.

    Waterproofing for Older Mississippi Homes in Meridian, MS

    Older basements were often built with block walls and no exterior membrane. Older homes in Mississippi often have galvanized supply lines and cast iron drains, both of which corrode and restrict flow with age. Water collects inside hollow block cores, and weep holes at the base let it drain into a perimeter channel. Clay tile drains fail too.

    Crack repairs use two-part polyurethane or epoxy injection resin, depending on whether the crack is actively wet. Polyurethane handles wet cracks. Interior drains use rigid perforated pipe in washed stone and a dimpled plastic membrane on the wall, which is more reliable than the flexible corrugated pipe some installers use because it holds its slope and can be jetted.

    Tightening a loose fitting or replacing a washer hose is a reasonable DIY task. Cracked drain stacks are not. Slab leaks and sump wiring need a pro, since a mistake can flood the floor or create a shock risk.

    Most supply and drain repairs are finished in a few hours. A new sump takes most of a day. Drain stack replacements and slab leak repairs usually take one to two days, since they involve opening finished surfaces, letting any new concrete patch cure, and running a final test before we close things up.

    Start outside. Keep gutters clean and extend downspouts at least six feet from the foundation. Grade the soil away from the house, test the sump with a bucket each spring, and insulate sweating cold lines. Check the water heater yearly.

    Plumbing leaks show up no matter the weather. Groundwater leaks track the rain. Turn off every fixture and watch the water meter, and if it still moves, the leak is in the pressurized lines, while water that only appears a few hours after a storm is almost always coming from outside.

    Check the base of the walls after a heavy rain. Dark damp bands, puddles at the cove joint or water near the floor drain are the usual signs. For plumbing leaks, turn off every faucet and appliance, then watch the water meter for 30 minutes, and if the dial or leak indicator moves, water is escaping somewhere in the system.
