    What Goes Into a Sewer Replacement in Greenville, MS

    Addressing Tree Root Intrusions in Greenville, MS

    Roots find sewer lines by following moisture. They need only a tiny gap. Once inside, they grow into a mat that catches paper and grease, and a root cutter clears them for a season at most, because the opening is still there. New PVC with glued joints gives them no way in.

    Advanced Diagnostics for Sewer Issues in Greenville, MS

    Diagnosis comes before price. Video tells us the problem and the sonde tells us the spot. We sometimes add a hydro-jet cleaning first, because a line full of grease can hide a crack, and a clean pipe gives a much more honest picture of its condition. Guesswork costs money.

    Open-Trench Sewer Replacement in Greenville, MS

    Trenching lets us fix grade. That is its big advantage. When an old line has a sag that holds water and solids, bursting would just follow the same sag, so we dig and reset the pipe on a proper bed at a proper fall. Bellies disappear.

    Clay and Orangeburg Pipe Replacement in Greenville, MS

    Clay pipe came in short sections with a joint every two or three feet. Each of those joints is an opening for roots. Orangeburg is worse, since the tar-paper wall flattens slowly under the dirt above it, and both are good candidates for bursting because they break apart easily. Fast pulls.

    Trenchless Sewer Replacement Options in Greenville, MS

    Bursting and pulling replace the pipe. CIPP lining coats the inside of the existing pipe instead. We use bursting when the old line is broken or undersized, because it leaves you with brand new pipe rather than a liner inside an old one, and lining when the pipe is still round and mostly intact. Each has its place.

    Traditional Trench Sewer Replacement in Greenville

    An open trench gives full access to the pipe from the house to the main. A small excavator does most of it, with hand digging near other lines. The old pipe comes out, the trench bottom gets a bed of gravel or sand, and new 4-inch PVC goes in at a steady quarter inch of fall per foot, confirmed with a level on every length. Then the camera.

    Cast Iron Sewer Replacement in Greenville

    Cast iron sewer pipe lasts a long time, but not forever. Over decades it scales up inside and the bottom wears through. We replace it with PVC, and where cast iron remains under the house we use a shielded no-hub coupling at the transition. Clean seal.

    Sewer Scope Inspections for Replacement in Greenville

    A sewer camera on a push cable goes in through the cleanout. It records every joint, crack and root on the way to the main. A small transmitter called a sonde sits behind the camera head, letting us mark the depth and location from the surface, which keeps the digging small. Bring questions.
