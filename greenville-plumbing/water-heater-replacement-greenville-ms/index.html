    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Replacement in Greenville

    The cost of water heater replacement in Greenville, MS typically ranges from $1,200 to $3,500, depending on the type of unit (gas, electric, tankless or hybrid), its capacity and any needed plumbing modifications. Our team will provide a detailed, upfront quote after assessing your needs and existing setup.

    Choosing the right size depends on the number of people in your household, their hot water usage habits and peak demand times. Our technicians will calculate the appropriate GPM (gallons per minute) for tankless or gallon capacity for tank models so you never run out of hot water, especially in multi-bathroom Greenville homes.

    Most replacements take two to four hours. Attic installs or relocations can take more time.

    New water heaters typically come with a manufacturer's warranty on the tank and parts, usually ranging from 6 to 12 years. DFM Plumbing also provides a labor warranty on our installation, giving you added peace of mind. Specific warranty details vary by brand and model.

    Upgrading provides more reliable hot water, improved energy efficiency and lower utility bills, better performance and a reduced risk of leaks or breakdowns. Newer models often have advanced features and longer warranties, improving comfort and safety in your home.

    While it might seem tempting, water heater replacement involves gas lines, high voltage electricity, water pressure and heavy equipment. It is a complex job best handled by licensed professionals to ensure safety, proper installation and compliance with local plumbing codes, preventing potential leaks or hazards.

    Flushing a traditional tank water heater once a year is recommended in most homes. Much of the water in Mississippi comes from underground aquifers and is fairly soft, though some supplies carry iron or a sulfur smell. This helps remove sediment buildup, which can reduce efficiency and shorten the lifespan of the unit. Regular flushing can also prevent scale from impacting heating elements.

    In general, if your water heater is over 10 years old, frequently breaks down or has a major issue like a leaking tank, replacement is often more cost-effective in the long run. For newer units with minor, easily fixable issues, repair might be a sensible option. Our technicians can provide an honest assessment.
