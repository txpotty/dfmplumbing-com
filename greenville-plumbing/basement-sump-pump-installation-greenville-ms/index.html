    Battery Backup Sump Pump Systems in Greenville

    Outages and heavy rain tend to show up together. Without house power, a battery backup keeps a second pump going, and a fully charged 75 amp-hour battery can run a typical backup pump for several hours of on-and-off cycling, longer on a light night. We test it under load.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Installation in Greenville, MS

    Battery backups earn their keep during outages. Flash flooding can overwhelm storm drains and push sewage up through sewer lines, particularly in older neighborhoods. A second pump goes into the same pit on its own float switch, wired to a sealed AGM battery on a smart charger that tests itself and beeps when the battery gets weak. The main pump keeps doing the daily work.

    Discharge Line and Check Valve Installation in Greenville

    Rigid PVC carries the water from the pump out through the rim joist and at least 10 feet from the house. A spring check valve stops the column from falling back into the pit, which cuts motor run time and the loud thunk you hear after every cycle. Wrapping exposed lines and letting faucets drip on the coldest nights prevent most of the damage a sudden freeze can cause. Where lines can freeze, we fit a freeze-resistant outlet.

    New Sump Pit Excavation in Greenville

    No pit yet? First we locate the low point where water gathers, saw cut the slab, dig roughly 24 to 30 inches deep, and set a liner surrounded by washed gravel so water can enter while fine soil stays out. New concrete goes back flush and level.

    Zoeller Sump Pump Installation in Greenville

    Zoeller's M53 Mighty-Mate is the cast iron pump we install more than any other. It is a 3/10 horsepower unit with a vertical float switch, and it moves about 2,580 gallons an hour at 5 feet of lift, enough for a lot of Greenville basements. Parts are easy to get.

    Pedestal Sump Pumps in Greenville

    Older homes often have narrow pits that will not fit a submersible. That is where a pedestal pump earns its place, and we install it with rigid discharge, a quality check valve, and a float rod set to your pit depth so it starts before water reaches the top of the liner. They are noisy but durable.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Installation in Greenville

    Pedestal pumps put the motor on a column above the water. That keeps it dry and easy to reach, which is why they suit narrow pits under 14 inches across, and why they often outlast cheap submersibles in Greenville homes. They are louder, though.

    Submersible Sump Pump Installation in Greenville, MS

    Submersible units sit down in the pit, sealed, with the water around them cooling the motor. You hear far less of them than a pedestal, and in Greenville, MS we set them on a couple of bricks so the intake stays above the silt that settles on the bottom of every liner. Sealed wiring matters here.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Greenville

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. A brand new pit through the slab falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and a battery backup adds roughly $600 to $1,500. Most people add the backup.

    Look for a pump that runs nonstop, short cycles, or does not start when the pit fills. Corrosion on the housing and a float that hangs up are warning signs, and if your basement flooded even though you have a pump, the pump or its switch was not up to the job. Over seven years old is suspect.

    Mostly cast iron pumps from Zoeller, Wayne, Liberty Pumps and Little Giant. All four have parts at local supply houses, which matters when a switch fails on a Sunday and you want the pump running again without waiting a week for an order to ship. We stock the common models.

    Flash flooding can overwhelm storm drains and push sewage up through sewer lines, particularly in older neighborhoods. Think of it as cheap insurance.

    Older homes in Mississippi often have galvanized supply lines and cast iron drains, both of which corrode and restrict flow with age. Slab homes without a basement rarely need a pump. Houses with basements or below-grade rooms are another story, since they can take on water fast during heavy storms, especially where the ground sheds rain rather than soaking it up. A cleanup costs more than a pit.

    Grinding or rattling during a cycle is the first warning. Rust on the housing and constant on-off cycling are others, along with a pump that does not start during a heavy rain even though the pit is full to the float. Past ten years, replace it.
