    Water Heater Safety Inspection in Greenville

    A water heater stores a lot of hot water under pressure, so safety items matter.     Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbing Inspection in Greenville

    Standard plumbing inspections usually cost $150 to $300. Adding a sewer camera inspection usually brings the total to $250 to $500.

    Most residential inspections in Greenville take one to two hours. Age and size matter. The exact time depends on how big the house is, how old the plumbing is and whether we find something that needs extra tracing, such as a hidden leak or a sewer defect.

    A general home inspector checks basic function, while a plumber goes deeper with sewer cameras, pressure tests and pipe identification. Plenty of buyers use both.

    We inspect all the common brands and materials. Heaters from every major maker are covered. Pipe types include PEX, copper, CPVC, PVC, cast iron, galvanized and polybutylene, and we know the typical failure points for each one along with the fixture brands found in most homes.

    We check water supply lines, drain lines, fixtures, the water heater, sump pump, garbage disposal and exposed gas lines. Drainage gets tested. Our plumber also checks for leaks, corrosion and vent issues, measures pressure and writes up the condition of every component.

    Watch for a water bill that jumps for no reason, low pressure, slow drains or brown water. Gurgling drains count too. Damp spots on walls or floors, a musty smell under a sink or a warm patch of tile can all point to a hidden leak that an inspection will find before it does more damage.

    Most homes do well with an inspection every one to two years. Before buying is a good time too. If you are purchasing a property, live in an older house or have had recent leaks or backups, a yearly check gives you a better chance of catching the next problem early.

    Every one to two years works for most homes. Older homes may need more. If your home has original supply pipe, large trees near the sewer or a history of leaks, a yearly inspection catches problems while they are still small and cheap to fix.
