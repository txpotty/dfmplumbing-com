    Common Toilet Repairs in Greenville, MS

    Flush Valve Seat Repair in Greenville

    Scale on the flush valve seat is the reason a brand-new flapper sometimes keeps leaking, because the rubber cannot seal against a rough, crusted ring. Feel the seat with a finger. A scrub pad and some vinegar clean most seats, and if it is pitted we install a seat repair kit or a new flush valve. Then we set the float so the water stops about an inch below the overflow.

    Clogged Toilet Clearing in Greenville, MS

    Most clogs sit in the trapway, the curved channel cast into the porcelain, and a closet auger with a rubber-sleeved head clears them without scratching the bowl. Plungers miss some. When the auger finds nothing and the bowl still fills, the blockage is further down the drain or in the main line. We run a cable from the cleanout or put a camera in.

    Toilet Flush Repair in Greenville, MS

    Two or three tries to get a full flush points to a flapper closing too early, a chain hooked in the wrong hole, scale in the rim jets, or a cracked overflow tube letting water bypass the bowl. Each one has a tell. Our plumber watches a complete flush with the tank lid off and replace the flush valve if the overflow tube is cracked, since it cannot be patched.

    Running Toilet Repair in Greenville

    A tank that keeps hissing and refilling every few minutes is almost always losing water past the flapper or overfilling into the overflow tube. It adds up fast. We look at the chain, float, flapper and seat before touching a part, then replace only what failed. Most running toilets are quiet again in about thirty minutes.

    Toilet Leaking Through the Ceiling in Greenville, MS

    Water coming through the ceiling under an upstairs bathroom usually traces back to a failed wax ring or a cracked supply nut, and the drywall below holds the evidence. Stop using that toilet. We pull the bowl, check the flange and reset it on a new seal, then we show you how far the water traveled so you can decide about the ceiling.

    Weak Flush and Clog Repair in Greenville, MS

    Needing two flushes on a regular basis usually comes down to scale in the rim jets, a clogged siphon jet, a partial blockage in the trapway, or a flush valve that does not open all the way. Gurgling in a nearby sink points to the vent. We clear the trap with an auger and treat the jets with a descaler. Some early 1.6 gallon toilets from the 1990s just flush poorly, and we will say so.

    After-Hours Toilet Repair in Greenville, MS

    Shutting off the angle stop buys you time, but an old multi-turn stop valve can crumble or leak at the stem when it has not been moved in fifteen years. Do not crank on it hard. If it will not close, the main shutoff for the house is the backup, and we can replace the stop with a quarter-turn valve once the water is off.

    Toilet Brands We Service in Greenville

    Kohler, American Standard, TOTO, Gerber and Mansfield make up most of the toilets we open, and each one uses its own flapper size or flush valve design. The wrong flapper will leak. We match the part to the model number stamped inside the tank and stock the common ones on every truck.

    A local plumber knows that a Greenville house built in the 1960s probably has a cast iron closet bend and a brass angle stop that has not been turned in decades. It changes how carefully we handle the shutoff. We bring a replacement stop valve on every toilet call in case the old one fails when we close it.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Repair in Greenville

    Expect $120 to $250 for a simple flapper or handle job and $200 to $500 for a base reset or a clog that goes past the trap into the drain line. A broken flange adds a bit more. We quote a flat price once we see the toilet, before any work begins.

    A failed wax ring is the usual cause, often helped along by a toilet that rocks or a flange that has cracked or rusted through. Condensation can fool you, though. On a humid day, the tank sweats and drips down the outside, which looks like a base leak but is not one.

    Constant running, a weak flush, water at the base and a bowl that rocks are the big four. A whistle or hiss from the tank usually means the fill valve is wearing out, and a hairline crack in the porcelain should be looked at before it spreads. Cracks are the one sign you should not sit on.

    Kohler, American Standard, TOTO, Gerber, Mansfield and Delta cover most of what we see, and we stock their common flappers and fill valves. We also work on older Eljer and Briggs toilets. Parts for some discontinued models take a day or two to arrive.

    Twenty to forty-five minutes covers most flapper, handle, fill valve and supply line jobs. Pulling and resetting the toilet runs about an hour, and if the flange is cracked we add thirty to sixty minutes for the repair ring or a new flange. We give you a time estimate up front.

    Repair makes sense when the porcelain is sound and the toilet flushes well with new parts. A cracked bowl or tank usually points to replacement, a toilet that clogs every week no matter what we do, or an old 3.5 gallon model that wastes water on every flush. You get a price for each option.

    Repeat clogs usually come from wipes, toys, too much paper per flush, or an early low-flow toilet that never moved waste well. Other times the trouble is further down the line, like a sagging drain, a crushed pipe, roots in the sewer or a blocked vent that keeps the bowl from draining with full force.
