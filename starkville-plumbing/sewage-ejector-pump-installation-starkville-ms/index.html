    Sump Pump vs. Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Starkville

    Sump pumps and ejector pumps look alike in a catalog but do very different jobs. A sump pump moves groundwater from a perimeter drain to the yard, while an ejector moves sewage from toilets and sinks to the sewer, and the ejector basin has to be sealed and vented because of the gas. They are not interchangeable.

    Basement Bathroom Ejector Systems in Starkville

    Basement bathrooms sit below the building sewer, so every fixture drains to a sealed pit instead of flowing by gravity. We saw the floor, trench drains to the basin, set it, carry a two-inch vent to the roof and run discharge to the main drain through a check valve and shutoff. The slab gets repoured.

    Liberty Pumps Installation in Starkville

    Liberty ejector pumps show up on a lot of our installs, with cast iron bodies and two-inch solids handling on most sewage models. Liberty sells packaged systems with basin, lid, pump and check valve already matched, which means less field fitting and a listed assembly for inspection. We still add a shutoff.

    Liberty Pumps Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Starkville, MS

    Liberty builds simplex and duplex packages that arrive with the basin, pump, lid and fittings matched. Matched packages speed up the install, and Liberty's float design is less likely to snag on the basin wall, a common reason older pumps fail to start. Switch failures cause many calls.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Basin & Pit Installation in Starkville

    Digging the pit means cutting the slab and excavating below it, deep enough to set the basin flush with the finished floor. Expansive clay soils are common in Mississippi, swelling with rain and shrinking in dry spells, which puts stress on foundations and buried pipes. We bed the basin on gravel and backfill around it so it cannot float when groundwater rises, then pour new concrete to seal the floor and lock the basin in place. A floating basin breaks pipe.

    New Construction Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Starkville, MS

    On a new build, we set the basin ahead of the slab pour. With no concrete to cut, it costs the least then, and we stub drains, vent and discharge to the plans for a clean rough-in inspection. It keeps the pour on schedule.

    Ejector Pump Replacement in Starkville

    Ejector pumps tend to run 7 to 10 years, depending on how often they cycle and what gets flushed. Watch for constant running, a hum or grind, repeated alarms and odor at the pit, since any of them means trouble is close. The float usually goes first.

    Residential Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Starkville

    Residential ejector systems in Starkville serve basement bathrooms, lower-level suites and any fixture that sits too low to drain by gravity to the sewer. The setup is a sealed basin, a pump, a float switch, a check valve and a vent. The parts are simple. Most problems come from the install, not the hardware, so we take our time on the vent and the lid seal.
