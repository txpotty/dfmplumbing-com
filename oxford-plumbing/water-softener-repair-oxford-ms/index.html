    Resin Bed Replacement in Oxford

    Chlorine eats resin slowly, and after enough years the beads swell, break and stop exchanging the calcium and magnesium they are supposed to hold. This is common with older systems on treated city water. DFM Plumbing pulls the old resin and fills the tank with new media. The softener works like new.

    Salt Bridge & Brine Tank Repair in Oxford

    Salt bridges form when a hard crust builds across the brine tank and the salt below it stops dissolving into the water. The tank looks full, but the softener cannot make brine. We break the bridge, clean out the mushy salt and check the float and brine well. A cracked brine tank gets swapped for a new one.

    Water Softener Leak Detection & Repair in Oxford, MS

    Cracked tanks, loose fittings and failed O-rings are behind most softener leaks. A small drip still wastes water and damages flooring. We find the source, whether it is the bypass, the valve-to-tank seal or the drain connection, and replace the seal or fitting that failed. We test it under pressure.

    Emergency Water Softener Repair in Oxford

    A softener stuck in regeneration can run water to the drain for hours or flood a utility room. We handle these calls quickly. The crew in Oxford puts the unit in bypass first, then finds out whether the valve, the timer or the drain line caused it. Most of the time it is a stuck piston or a failed motor that can be replaced on the same visit.

    Bypass and Fitting Leak Repair in Oxford, MS

    Bypass valves, the O-rings where the valve meets the tank, cracked fittings and the drain line are the usual leak points. A minor drip still wastes water and damages floors and cabinets. We replace O-rings or fittings and test under pressure. Mineral tank cracks cannot be fixed.

    Softener Repair vs Replacement Cost in Oxford, MS

    Most softener repairs cost $150 to $450, while a resin replacement often runs $400 to $900 depending on tank size. A new quality softener installed typically costs $1,500 to $3,500.     Softener Not Regenerating in Oxford

    Hard water coming back usually means the softener has stopped regenerating. The usual causes are a dead timer motor, a bad board, a stuck meter or settings wiped by an outage. We test each part. Then we program the valve for your household and hardness, and soft water returns by the next cycle.

    Stuck pistons, worn seals and failed drive motors cause most control valve trouble, and the symptoms are hard water, constant regeneration or a leak at the head. The valve gets tested in place. Our plumbers carry seal kits and pistons for Fleck, Clack and Autotrol heads, which covers a big share of the softeners installed in homes around here.
