    Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Oxford, MS

    Electric tankless heaters make sense in Oxford, MS homes without gas, or for a single sink or a guest bath far from the main heater. A whole-home electric unit draws heavy amperage. Some need two or three 40 to 60 amp double-pole breakers, and a 100-amp panel often cannot carry that on top of the air conditioner and range. We check the panel first.

    Navien Tankless Water Heater Installation in Oxford

    Navien builds condensing tankless units with stainless heat exchangers, and many models include a built-in recirculation pump. The built-in pump can move hot water toward a distant bath on a timer, cutting the wait at the tap without a separate pump. Venting is usually two-inch or three-inch PVC. There is a condensate line to route as well.

    Propane Tankless Water Heater Installation in Oxford

    Propane homes in Oxford can run tankless units too, using a model built or converted for propane. Since propane runs at different pressure from natural gas, the regulator, line sizing and gas valve must all match, and the supplier may need to swap the tank regulator. A small tank can run short.

    Gas Tankless Water Heater Installation in Oxford

    Gas tankless units in Oxford are the common pick for whole-house hot water because a single 199,000 BTU burner can feed two showers and a sink at the same time without losing temperature. The catch is gas supply. Most homes need the line upsized from the meter, often from half-inch to three-quarter inch or larger. We size it and pressure test it before the unit is lit.

    Tankless vs Tank Cost Comparison in Oxford, MS

    Upfront, a tankless install in Oxford, MS costs more than a standard tank because of the unit price, the gas line upgrade and the venting changes. Over the long haul, the math narrows. Less standby loss, a life of up to 20 years versus roughly 10 for a tank, and replaceable parts all count over the years. We price both side by side.

    Residential Tankless Water Heater Installation in Oxford

    Sizing a home unit comes down to two numbers: peak gallons per minute and temperature rise. A shower head flows about 2 gallons per minute and a kitchen faucet a bit less, so a house that runs two showers and a sink together needs roughly 5 to 6 gallons at the rise your inlet water calls for. That narrows the model list fast.

    Residential Tankless System Upgrades in Oxford

    Upgrading from a tank in Oxford starts with a look at what you have now: tank size, vent type, gas line size and where the unit sits. In some homes the old flue gets capped and the new unit vents out a side wall in PVC, which is simpler and opens the old chase. Other homes need a longer run. The quote says which.

    Commercial Tankless Water Heater Installation in Oxford

    Commercial hot water loads in Oxford are heavier and more constant than residential. A restaurant can run a dish machine, hand sinks and a mop sink together, and the rack must hold temperature through the rush. We rack units with a common manifold. Service one, keep the others going.
