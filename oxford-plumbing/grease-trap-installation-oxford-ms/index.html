    What Goes Into a Grease Trap Install in Oxford, MS

    Hydromechanical Grease Trap Installation in Oxford

    Flow rating is the number that matters on a hydromechanical trap, and it has to match the fastest-draining fixture combination in your kitchen. Put a 50 GPM unit on a 75 GPM load and grease goes out the back at every rush. We do the math on paper. Each sink compartment's volume, the drain size and a one or two minute drain time give us the GPM figure the code uses.

    Residential Grease Interceptor Setup in Oxford, MS

    Homes rarely need a grease trap, though some do. We see them in licensed home kitchens and in houses where the sewer lateral keeps clogging with grease. A small 4 to 7 GPM unit under the kitchen sink catches most of it, and cleaning takes about ten minutes with a spatula and a bucket. Check with your local health office first.

    Restaurant Grease Trap Compliance in Oxford, MS

    Most sewer authorities run a FOG program with an inspector who opens your trap and checks your pump-out manifests against the schedule they set. A failed inspection can bring a fine or an order to upgrade. We install to pass. We add a sampling port if they require it and size to the authority's own formula.

    New Restaurant Build-Out Grease Traps in Oxford

    On a new build-out, the grease trap gets decided before the slab is poured, not after. We work from the architect's plumbing sheets and the kitchen equipment schedule. Changes get expensive. Moving a trap after the concrete cures means cutting the floor, so we catch problems while it is still paper.

    Hydro-Mechanical Grease Interceptor Installation in Oxford

    Inside a hydromechanical interceptor, baffles do the separating while a flow control fitting with an air intake holds incoming water to the rated flow. The air intake has to be vented to code, not left open in the kitchen. Get that wrong and the trap siphons. We also set the unit level within a quarter inch, since a tilted trap short-circuits the baffles and loses capacity.

    Commercial Kitchen Grease Trap Installs in Oxford

    Every commercial install starts with a fixture list. The tech walks the kitchen and writes down every fixture that drains to the trap and how fast each one empties. That number decides between an indoor unit and an outdoor tank, and where it can go with clean pipe runs to the sewer. Fewer bends, fewer clogs.

    Replacement and Upgrade Services in Oxford, MS

    Steel traps from decades back rust through at the baffle seams, and by then the outlet is usually passing grease straight to the sewer. We run a camera down the outlet first to see how much grease got past the old unit. Sometimes the line needs jetting too. Upgrading also gives you a chance to move from a 20 GPM trap to a 35 or 50 if the menu or seat count grew.

    Gravity Grease Trap Installation in Oxford

    A gravity interceptor works on time, not machinery. Wastewater slows down in the tank long enough for grease to float and solids to settle. Sizing comes from your fixture units and the retention time your sewer authority wants, and we check that number with them before ordering a tank. No moving parts to fail.
