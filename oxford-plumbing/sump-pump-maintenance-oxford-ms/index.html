    Check Valve and Discharge Pipe Repair in Oxford, MS

    Commercial Sump Pump Maintenance in Oxford, MS

    Commercial pits are often bigger and deeper, and many have two pumps on an alternating controller. We test both pumps, the alternator and the high-water alarm. You get a written record of each reading, which comes in handy for insurance renewals and inspections. Quarterly visits are common.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Maintenance in Oxford, MS

    Backup pumps sit in the pit next to the main pump and run off a 12-volt battery when the power drops or the primary fails. We check the battery with a load tester, not just a voltmeter. Then we unplug the main pump and let the backup empty the pit on its own. Alarms get tested too.

    Crawlspace and Window Well Pumps in Oxford, MS

    Not every sump sits in a basement. Crawlspaces and deep window wells often have small pumps that clear runoff after a storm. We clean their screens, test the floats and follow the discharge until it reaches daylight or an approved drain, because a line that dead-ends under the house just moves the water around. Wrapping exposed lines and letting faucets drip on the coldest nights avoid most of the damage a sudden freeze can cause.

    Zoeller Sump Pump Maintenance in Oxford

    Zoeller pumps like the M53 and M63 use cast iron housings and an integral float switch, and they tend to outlast cheaper plastic units. Even so, we look for cracks in the casting and make sure the float moves freely. We carry the common Zoeller parts on the truck.

    Home Sump Pump Service in Oxford

    A typical house pit is about 18 inches across and two feet deep, and most of what goes wrong happens inside it.     Pre-Storm Season Pump Testing in Oxford

    Flash flooding can overwhelm storm drains and push sewage up through sewer lines, particularly in older neighborhoods.     Submersible Sump Pump Maintenance in Oxford

    Submersible pumps sit at the bottom of the pit, which keeps them quiet but hides the problems. Our plumber pulls the unit out so we can see the cord and the shaft seal. A cracked cord usually means a new pump. The intake screen gets cleaned, and we look over the housing for corrosion, since pumps sitting in standing water for years rust from the bottom up.
