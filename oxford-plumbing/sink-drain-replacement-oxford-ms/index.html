    Pop-Up Drain Replacement in Oxford

    Lift-rod pop-ups are the most common bath drain. We pull the old body, clean the flange seat down to bare porcelain, reset the new flange in putty or silicone, and adjust the clevis so the lift rod opens and closes the stopper with one easy motion. We match the faucet finish. Overflow-style drains are standard.

    Bathroom Sink Drain Replacement in Oxford, MS

    Bathroom drains wear out at the pop-up. Pivot nuts leak and drain bodies corrode at the overflow holes, so a sink that will not hold water needs a new pop-up. Patching one piece rarely works. The new drain matches your faucet finish.

    Kitchen Sink Drain Replacement in Oxford

    Kitchen drains start failing at the basket strainer. Old strainer putty dries out and the locknut rusts, so water runs down the tailpiece and looks like a trap leak. Strainer and trap get swapped as a pair. Disposal sinks get a new flange instead.

    Kitchen Drain and Disposal Tie-In in Oxford

    Disposals change the kitchen drain. The outlet on the disposal has to sit above the trap inlet, the dishwasher hose needs a high loop or an air gap, and the trap has to line up without the slip joints pulling sideways. They all get set together. Then the disposal runs with water while we watch.

    P-Trap Replacement in Oxford

    Thin chrome traps are the ones we replace most. They rust from the inside, the plating flakes off, and a slip nut that will not loosen often twists the whole trap apart, so we cut it out and install heavy-wall PVC or polished brass that matches the room. The new trap gets a full flow test. Total time is often under an hour.

    Vessel and Grid Drain Replacement in Oxford

    Vessel sinks use a different drain. Most glass and stone vessel bowls have no overflow hole, so they need an unslotted grid or umbrella drain, and a slotted drain in one of those bowls leaks out of the slots right into the counter hole. Mismatched drains are common. Swapping it takes under an hour.

    Trap Arm and Wall Drain Repair in Oxford, MS

    Leaks right at the wall point to the trap arm or stub. A cracked arm or rusted stub drips down the back panel, out of sight, until the wood swells or smells. The bad section gets cut back. Then new PVC gets tied in.

    Hard-Water Drain Damage Repair in Oxford, MS

    Iron can leave orange stains on fixtures and laundry, and sulfur odors often trace back to the water heater anode or the well itself. Scale grows inside pop-up bodies and on stopper rods until the linkage seizes. Forcing a scaled lift rod usually snaps the pivot and leaves the stopper stuck, with a narrowed tailpiece slowing the drain. The scaled parts all come out. Smooth PVC sheds buildup better.
