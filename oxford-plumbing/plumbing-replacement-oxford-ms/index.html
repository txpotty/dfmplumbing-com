    Water heaters in Oxford that leak from the tank, rumble on every heat cycle or run out of hot water halfway through a shower are usually cheaper to replace than to keep repairing.     Water Softener Replacement in Oxford, MS

    Softeners wear out as the resin breaks down and the control valve stops cycling properly. Much of the water in Mississippi comes from underground aquifers and is fairly soft, though some supplies carry iron or a sulfur smell. Once scale returns, dishes spot or salt stops dropping, we test hardness, size a new unit and plumb it with a bypass so the house keeps water during service. New valves use less salt.

    Water Line Replacement in Oxford, MS

    A leaking water service line runs your meter all day and night and can soften the soil next to the foundation. In Oxford, MS, we swap old galvanized, poly or failing copper service lines for PEX or copper from meter to house, plus a new full-port main shutoff. One day covers most.

    Repair vs Replacement Cost Comparison in Oxford, MS

    A common rule of thumb is to replace when a repair costs more than half the price of new, or when the part is near the end of its life.     Pipe Replacement & Whole-Home Repiping in Oxford, MS

    Homes in Oxford, MS with galvanized steel or polybutylene usually show it the same ways: low pressure, rusty water and leaks that keep coming back.     Sump Pump and Well Pump Replacement in Oxford, MS

    A dead sump pump during a storm floods a basement fast, and a dead well pump means no water at all. In Oxford, MS, sump pumps get sized for inflow with a check valve and an optional battery backup, while well pumps get matched to depth, flow and switch settings. We run each one through cycles.

    Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Oxford

    A well pump that clicks on and off constantly usually points to a failed pressure tank. When the bladder inside the tank ruptures, the tank fills with water, loses its air cushion and forces the pump to start every time someone opens a tap, which burns out pump motors years early. Replacing the tank stops it. The pressure switch gets checked too.
