    Salt Usage and Settings Repair in Pearl

    Too much salt or water use usually means settings that do not match your hardness or household. An old timer head regenerates by the calendar, used or not. After retesting hardness, we reprogram the valve or swap in a metered head that regenerates only when needed. Salt bills drop.

    Brine Tank and Salt Bridge Repair in Pearl, MS

    A crust of salt across the brine tank stops regeneration, and hard water shows up at every tap within a day or two. The bridge gets broken up and the tank cleaned out. Cracked brine tanks on older units get replaced. Keep the salt about half full.

    Control Valve Troubleshooting and Repair in Pearl, MS

    Stuck pistons, worn seals and failed drive motors cause most control valve trouble, and the symptoms are hard water, constant regeneration or a leak at the head. We diagnose the valve on site. Seal kits and pistons for Fleck, Clack and Autotrol valves ride on the truck, so most rebuilds happen on the first visit.

    Commercial Water Softener Repair in Pearl, MS

    Laundromats and restaurants in Pearl, MS run through soft water fast, and a dishwasher or boiler starts scaling the moment the softener stops working. Downtime costs money.     Resin Testing and Replacement in Pearl

    Resin beads trap hardness minerals and release them during regeneration. Chlorine and chloramine in city water slowly break resin down until it turns to mush. We test resin capacity, then clean it with resin cleaner or replace it, and a solid tank and valve with fresh resin works like new for years. Mushy resin can clog the valve.

    Water Softener Leak Detection and Repair in Pearl, MS

    Cracked tanks, loose fittings and failed O-rings are behind most softener leaks. A small drip still wastes water and damages flooring. We find the source, whether it is the bypass, the valve-to-tank seal or the drain connection, and replace the seal or fitting that failed. Then it gets a pressure test.

    Control Valve Rebuild in Pearl

    Service, backwash, brine and rinse are the four cycles the control valve directs every time the softener regenerates, usually in the middle of the night. Hard water and chlorine wear out the seals, spacers and pistons. Leaks at the valve or water running to the drain are common signs. The valve gets rebuilt with the kit made for it.
