    Pinhole Leak Repair in Pearl

    Pinholes start inside the pipe where the copper wall pits and thins. You see a drip or a stain. They are small and easy to miss behind drywall, which is why we use thermal imaging to find them before we open anything. We replace the pitted length with Type L. We look at the copper around it.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Copper Pipe Leak Repair in Pearl

    A minor, accessible pinhole repair starts around $200. Slab leaks run higher. Complex repairs like under-slab leaks can run past $2,000, with location, severity and footage of new pipe setting most of the price. You get a written number after locating.

    All standard copper used in homes and businesses. Type M, L and K. Our repairs use copper and fittings from established manufacturers that meet ASTM and NSF standards, and press fittings come from major brands built for potable water. No off-brand fittings.

    Wet spots on walls, ceilings or floors are the obvious sign. Pressure drops count too. The sound of running water when nothing is on, a bill that jumps, or blue-green crust on exposed fittings all point to copper that is leaking or about to. Check the meter with everything off.

    Water chemistry is a big factor. Homes around Pearl on wells or small rural water systems should test their water before choosing a filter. Pressure matters too. Water that is too acidic or too alkaline, aggressive soil around buried pipe, high pressure, poor installation and foundation movement each wear copper down or pull fittings apart. It is usually a combination.

    Wall thickness. Type K is the heaviest. Type K is used for underground service lines, Type L is the standard for interior water lines in many codes, while Type M is lighter and more prone to pinholes over time. We repair with Type L.

    Keep water pressure under about 80 psi. A regulator helps. Treating hard or aggressive water, insulating pipe in cold spots and limiting how long a recirculation pump runs slow down the corrosion that thins copper. Know where your main shutoff is.

    A repair clamp or epoxy can buy time in an emergency. It will not last. A plumber should cut out the damaged section and install proper fittings, particularly when the leak is somewhere you cannot see. Close the main before you try.

    Keep pressure in range. Test it yearly. Soften hard water, limit recirculation pump run time and get any green crust on fittings looked at early, while homes with older copper should have it checked every few years. Early repairs cost less.
