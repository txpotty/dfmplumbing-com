    Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Pearl

    A waterlogged pressure tank makes a healthy pump look sick. Short cycling, where the pump starts every few seconds of water use, usually means the tank bladder has failed and the pump is paying for it. We check the air charge with a gauge. The air charge should read 2 psi under the switch cut-in.

    Well Pump Sizing in Pearl

    Sizing starts with the well log. We get depth and static level from the well log or a sounder, then a draw-down test shows what the well can really produce in gallons per minute. Pump more than the well yields and it runs dry.

    Well Pump Electrical Troubleshooting in Pearl, MS

    No-water calls often end at the control box, not the pump. On a three-wire submersible, the start capacitor or the relay in that box fails far more often than the motor down the well does. Those get tested first. A $60 part beats a $2,500 pump pull.

    Jet Pump Suction Line Repair in Pearl, MS

    Foot valves and suction lines cause most jet pump trouble, not the pump. A pinhole in the suction pipe lets air in, the pump loses prime, and it runs dry until the seal burns up. We pressure test the line. A well that is too deep for suction needs a submersible, and we will quote that instead.

    Solar Well Pump Systems in Pearl, MS

    Solar well pumps make sense for livestock tanks and cabins where running power from the meter would cost more than the pump itself. A DC pump and a controller run off a few panels during daylight. You store water, not electricity. Panel count comes from how many gallons you need a day.

    Franklin Electric Well Pump Replacement in Pearl

    Franklin Electric builds a large share of the submersible motors sold in the US, including ones sitting under other brands' pump ends. Replacement control boxes are easy to source. We match the motor and the wire gauge to the depth of your well.

    Pump and Pressure Tank Matching in Pearl

    Pump and tank work as a pair. An undersized or waterlogged tank forces dozens of extra starts a day, and each start puts heat into the motor windings. We check it on every pump job.
