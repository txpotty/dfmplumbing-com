    Non-Invasive Slab Leak Locating in Pearl, MS

    The floor gets opened last.     Supply and Drain Leaks Under the Slab in Pearl, MS

    Supply leaks and drain leaks act differently. Supply leaks are pressurized, so they move the meter, warm the floor on the hot side and run whether or not anyone uses water. Drain leaks only leak when water goes down, and they show up as odors, slow drains or damp floors near bathrooms. Each needs its own test in your Pearl, MS home.

    Post-Tension Slab Leak Detection in Pearl

    A lot of newer houses are built on post-tension slabs. These slabs have steel tendons stretched under tens of thousands of pounds of force, and cutting one by mistake is dangerous and can damage the foundation. We scan for tendons with ground-penetrating radar or a rebar locator before opening anything, or recommend a reroute that never touches the concrete.

    Acoustic Slab Leak Detection in Pearl

    Water escaping a pressurized pipe makes a hiss that carries up through concrete. Our ground microphones hear it. We move the pickup across the floor in a grid over the traced pipe route, finding where the sound is loudest and confirming it from another position, which works best on copper and less well on PEX.

    Tracer Gas Leak Detection in Pearl

    PEX and CPVC do not carry sound well. For those, we drain the line and push in a safe hydrogen and nitrogen tracer gas, which rises through the slab and gets picked up by a sniffer at the floor surface, often through grout joints and hairline cracks. It catches leaks the microphone cannot in Pearl homes.

    Slab Leaks in Older Homes in Pearl, MS

    Older homes in Pearl, MS often have soft copper or galvanized steel under the slab, and both wear out with time. Copper fails at bends and where it rubs concrete, while galvanized rusts from the inside until it splits. Our techs know where these fail. That knowledge points the search at the likeliest spots first.

    Direct Access Slab Leak Repair in Pearl, MS

    One leak on healthy pipe is a good spot-repair candidate. We cut a small opening at the marked spot, dig down to the pipe, repair the section with approved fittings and pressure test it. Then we backfill and patch the concrete level, ready for flooring. Accurate marks keep that hole small.
