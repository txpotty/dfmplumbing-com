    Frequently Asked Questions About Residential Plumbing Service in Jackson

    Pricing depends on the job. Simple repairs like a faucet cartridge or toilet rebuild tend to cost $150 to $350, a water heater installation is often $1,200 to $3,500, and slab leak repairs or full repipes can reach several thousand dollars. You get an exact quote after we diagnose.

    A slab leak is a break in a water line running under a concrete foundation. Slab and crawlspace foundations are typical, and soil movement under a slab can shift drain lines and supply pipes. The usual signs are a warm patch of floor, a water bill that jumps or water running when nothing is on. Do not wait on it.

    Insulate any pipe in an unheated space, like a crawlspace, attic or garage wall. Unhook hoses from the spigots and open sink cabinets on exterior walls so warm air reaches the pipes, and on the coldest nights let a faucet drip at a slow, steady pace. If you will be away, shut off the main. Exposed pipes in crawlspaces, outdoor spigots and well equipment are the first to fail during a rare deep cold snap.

    Every one to two years works for most homes in Jackson, MS. A visit takes about an hour, and we check pressure, look for slow leaks at shutoffs and supply lines, test the water heater relief valve and look for corrosion on exposed pipe. Small finds cost far less to fix.

    Hard water carries dissolved calcium and magnesium. Soft water has had most of those minerals removed, usually by an ion-exchange softener, and the difference shows up as scale in water heaters, white crust on faucets, spotty dishes and soap that will not lather well. Soft water is easier on fixtures.

    Our plumbers install and service Moen, Delta, Kohler, American Standard, TOTO, Rheem, Bradford White and Navien, among others. We will tell you which ones hold up in your water, since cartridges in some brands handle hard water better than others. Some cost more but last longer.

    Soft water and normal pressure prevent a lot of what we see. Keep grease out of the drains and test pressure once a year, since anything over 80 psi wears out supply hoses and fixtures faster than people expect. A yearly inspection catches the rest.
