    Gas Valve and Thermostat Repair in Jackson, MS

    Lukewarm showers and a tank that never catches up often trace back to the gas control. We check the thermostat setting, the immersion sensor and the valve's outlet pressure with a manometer. On most residential tanks the thermostat lives inside the gas valve body, so a bad sensor or a stuck magnet usually means replacing the whole control rather than one small part. That swap takes about an hour. Every joint on the new valve gets leak tested.

    Pilot Light and Ignition Repair in Jackson

    No hot water and a dark pilot window usually point to the ignition side of the heater. We pull the pilot assembly and look at the thermocouple tip, the pilot orifice and the piezo igniter wire. A thermocouple that tests weak on the millivolt meter will not hold the gas valve's magnet open, so the pilot drops out a minute after you relight it by the label. Common pilot kits ride on most of our trucks.

    Gas Control Valve Replacement in Jackson, MS

    Honeywell and White-Rodgers valves report faults with a blinking LED, and the flash count matches a chart on the label. We read the code, then test the thermopile and sensors before blaming the valve. Valves do fail. When the control itself is the problem, we replace it with an exact-match valve or a listed universal model, set the temperature and leak test every joint on the gas train before relighting. You get the old valve back if you want it.

    Burner Cleaning and Repair in Jackson, MS

    Yellow flame tips, soot on the burner door or a roaring sound at startup all mean the burner is not getting clean air. The burner comes out, the ports get brushed and the orifice gets cleared. We also confirm the orifice is drilled for the fuel you have, because a natural gas orifice on a propane heater, or the reverse, will soot up the flue and push carbon monoxide levels well past normal. A good flame is blue and steady. Rust flakes on the burner tell us to look up the flue.

    Pilot Light Repair in Jackson

    The thermocouple is the first thing we test when a pilot will not stay lit. A millivolt meter tells us in about a minute whether it is producing enough current. If it reads fine, the next suspects are a clogged pilot orifice, a cracked piezo wire or a thermal cutoff that tripped because the combustion chamber overheated from blocked air. Figure on roughly an hour for most pilot repairs.
